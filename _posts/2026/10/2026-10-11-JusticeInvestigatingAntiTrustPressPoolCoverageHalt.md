## [DOJ says it's investigating whether White House TV pool's coverage halt violated antitrust law](https://www.ms.now/news/doj-white-house-tv-pool-coverage-inverstigation)

_Last month, the television pool of five broadcasters ceased [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)ial coverage after the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) barred [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/), [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) and [Politico](https://www.politico.com/) from the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)._

By [The Associated Press](https://www.ms.now/author/the-associated-press)

Oct. 10, 2026, 10:50 PM EDT

[WASHINGTON](https://dc.gov/) ([AP](https://apnews.com/)) — The U.S. [Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/) is investigating whether [decision of the White House television](https://apnews.com/article/trump-pool-ban-cnn-msnow-politico-dc1a144d37a5d2e90488eec110ca9835) press pool to halt coverage of [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) last month violated antitrust law, an agency spokesperson said Saturday.

[Emily Covington](https://www.linkedin.com/in/emily-covington-1b18938/), a spokesperson for the department, said group boycotts among business competitors might violate U.S. law. “The [Antitrust Division](https://www.justice.gov/atr) is simply investigating to determine whether these news organizations have violated the antitrust laws,” she said.

The announcement came after the television pool of five broadcasters last month ceased [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) coverage after the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) barred [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/), [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) and [Politico](https://www.politico.com/) from the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/).

Messages were sent late Saturday to the five outlets seeking comment: [ABC](https://abc.com/), [CBS](https://www.cbs.com/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/), [FOX](https://www.fox.com/) and [NBC](https://www.nbc.com/).

The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) press pool, which is a rotating cast of news outlets that covers the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), has become a proxy for the wider fight over whether the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), or anyone, can control the content of news reports — a type of free speech, pillar of American democracy.

Last month’s developments marked an extraordinary escalation in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s efforts to restrict news reporters and outlets that he finds insufficiently supportive.

[CNN](https://www.cnn.com/), [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) and [Politico](https://www.politico.com/) sued over the [days-long ban](https://apnews.com/article/trump-media-ban-judge-ruling-cnn-msnow-politico-21605e397fe48f178a4afa037e732c6d). It was lifted after a judge handed the three news organizations a reprieve, which is in place until Tuesday.

Television pool coverage of the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has also resumed.

[Jose Zamora](https://cpj.org/about/people/), the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://cpj.org/)’ regional director of the Americas, on Saturday criticized the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/)’s decision to investigate.

“The government should not use antitrust investigations to intimidate news outlets defending press access,” [Zamora](https://cpj.org/about/people/) said on X. “The public’s right to know depends on that access.”
