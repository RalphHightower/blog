## [White House says MAGA Inc. will pay for taxpayer-funded ads after backlash](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-says-hell-personally-pay-for-taxpayer-funded-ads-after-bipartisan-backlash)

_The announcement comes after [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) joined [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) in questioning whether taxpayers should foot the bill for campaign-style ads._

By [Ebony Davis](https://www.ms.now/author/ebony-davis) and [Soorin Kim](https://www.ms.now/author/soorin-kim)

Oct. 5, 2026, 10:01 PM EDT

[President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s super PAC will pay for his campaign-style television ads moving forward, a [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) official told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) on Monday, shifting the cost away from taxpayers after the government-funded ad drew bipartisan backlash.

The clarification came hours after [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) said in a [Truth Social post](https://truthsocial.com/@realDonaldTrump/posts/117390649505706640) that he would stop using taxpayer dollars to fund the ads and suggested he could pay for them himself or with money raised by [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) Asked whether [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) intended to use his personal money or his super PAC funds, a [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) official told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) that [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) will pay for the ads “moving forward.”

[Facing bipartisan backlash](https://www.ms.now/news/republicans-criticize-trump-public-service-announcements), [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) defended the government-funded ads as he announced the change.

“The Radical Left is upset with the fact that I am taking Ads, which I consider to be a positive promotion for our Great U.S.A., and paying for them with U.S.A. money,” [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) wrote.

The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) called the use of taxpayer money for the ads “a rather standard thing to do,” but said he had nevertheless decided to pay for what he called the “Patriotic Ads” himself or use funds from [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/)

[MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) enters the final stretch of the midterm elections with [substantial resources](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/21/trump-maga-midterm-spending-war-chest.html): The super PAC had nearly \$416 million in cash on hand as of Aug. 31, according to its latest [Federal Election Commission](https://www.fec.gov/) filing.

Representatives for [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) did not immediately respond to [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/)’s request for comment.

The announcement follows [mounting scrutiny](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-propaganda-taxpayer-funds-election-campaign-ads-midterms) of the ads, which have aired nationally ahead of the midterm elections. The commercials feature [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and promote themes closely associated with his political agenda, including opposition to communism and what he calls the “deep state.”

The ads include disclaimers saying they were paid for by the U.S. government. It is unclear when [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) will begin paying for the ads or when taxpayer funding for them will end.

The [Department of Homeland Security](https://www.dhs.gov/) [set aside \$20 million](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/homeland-security-spending-20m-in-taxpayer-funds-for-pro-trump-ads) for the advertising campaign, using money [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) had appropriated to U.S. [Customs and Border Protection](https://www.cbp.gov/). More than [\$10 million](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/05/us/politics/trump-taxpayer-funded-ads-pac.html) worth of taxpayer-funded ads have aired since September, according to [AdImpact](https://adimpact.com/).

The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has justified the ads as [public service announcements](https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-defends-spending-millions-taxpayer-funded-promotional-ads/story?id=136922737) intended to promote patriotism and argued that previous administrations have used government-funded advertisements to promote policies and programs.

But the ads drew rare bipartisan criticism.

[Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) [Majority Leader](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/majority-minority-leaders.htm) [John Thune (T-SD](https://www.thune.senate.gov/) said last week that, while he liked the ads’ message, “it shouldn’t be paid for with taxpayer dollars.” [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [John Kennedy (T-LA)](https://www.kennedy.senate.gov/) said public officials should not spend government money on advertisements promoting themselves.

[Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) lawmakers have gone further, [raising questions](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/democratic-lawmakers-seek-probe-us-government-ads-featuring-trump-2026-10-01/) about whether the advertisements violate federal restrictions on using government funds for propaganda or political purposes. Ethics and legal experts [have also questioned](https://www.wmur.com/article/are-trumps-tv-ads-a-public-service-experts-say-no/73945325) the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) comparing the ads to traditional public service announcements, which typically inform the public about government programs or services.

[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s decision does not end the ads themselves. Instead, it shifts their financing away from government funds and toward private political money, addressing one of the central concerns raised by critics as the midterms inch closer.
