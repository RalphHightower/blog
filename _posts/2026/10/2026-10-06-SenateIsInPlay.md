## [Republicans invest in South Carolina as the definition of ‘longshot’ changes](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/midterm-elections-senate-south-carolina-kansas-mississippi-nebraska)

_It’s reached the point at which [Donald [lTrump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) sees [Dr. Annie Andrews](https://www.drannieandrews.com/) as worth attacking, which speaks volumes about her race._

By [Steve Benen](https://www.ms.now/author/steve-benen)

Oct. 6, 2026, 1:13 PM EDT

Ahead of a Monday night campaign rally, [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) published [an ugly missive](https://www.trumpstruth.org/statuses/42169) to his social media platform, using an anti-transgender slur to go after [Dr. Annie Andrews](https://www.drannieandrews.com/), the [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) U.S. [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) candidate in [South Carolina](https://www.sc.gov/).

In his online item, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) repeatedly referred to the pediatrician as “trannie” for reasons he did not explain. (It would appear [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) concluded that the rhyme with [Andrews](https://www.drannieandrews.com/)’ first name was sufficient justification for him to run with the slur.)

And while that was offensive and ridiculous, there is a silver lining for [Andrews](https://www.drannieandrews.com/) and her campaign: With a month to go before Election Day, her candidacy has reached the point at which she’s worth attacking. In fact, as of the latest round of campaign filings, [Andrews](https://www.drannieandrews.com/) has [actually raised more campaign funds](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/04/us/politics/south-carolina-senate-darline-graham-annie-andrews-donald-trump.html) than [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Darline Graham (T-SC)](https://www.dgraham.senate.gov/), the appointed [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) incumbent.

The morning after the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s obnoxious statement, [Axios reported](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/06/south-carolina-senate-gop-graham-money):

> The main super PAC supporting [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) candidates plans to inject \$5 million into the [South Carolina](https://www.sc.gov/) campaign as an “insurance policy” to help [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) nominee [Darline Graham](https://www.dgraham.senate.gov/), a source familiar with the move tells [Axios](https://www.axios.com/).

> The [GOP](https://www.gop.com/)’s concern that a [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) seat in blood-red [South Carolina](https://www.sc.gov/) could be in play shows just how bad the political environment is for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/), who face a rash of troubles in states long considered conservative strongholds.

In early April, the [Senate Leadership Fund](https://senateleadershipfund.org/) [announced](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/key-republican-super-pac-eyes-eight-senate-races-that-will-dictate-control-of-the-chamber) that it would spend nearly \$350 million in what it considered the most competitive [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) races. [The list](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/midterm-elections-republicans-democrats-congress-2026) featured seven predictable contests: [Ohio](https://ohio.gov/), [North Carolina](https://www.nc.gov/), [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/som), [Georgia](https://georgia.gov/), [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/portal/index.html), [Iowa](https://www.iowa.gov/), [New Hampshire](https://www.nh.gov/) and [Alaska](https://alaska.gov/).

Six months later, the same [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) super PAC is starting to [give up on North Carolina](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/texas-paxton-polls-kansas-alabama-georgia-north-carolina-ohio-alaska-midterm-elections), deeming it a lost cause; [investing in Texas](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/paxton-texas-talarico-senate-race-midterm-elections-campaign-republicans-millions), fearing a possible defeat; and starting to invest millions of dollars in [South Carolina](https://www.sc.gov/) — a state [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) won by 18 points two years ago and which hasn’t had a competitive [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) general election in the 21st century.

This is remarkable in its own right, but it’s also emblematic of a larger trend: With four weeks remaining in the midterm cycle and the wind apparently at [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/)’ backs, the definition of the word “longshot” is changing rather quickly.

In [Kansas](https://portal.kansas.gov/), not only is there recent polling that shows [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Roger Marshall (T-KS)](https://www.marshall.senate.gov/) [trailing his Democratic rival](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/texas-paxton-polls-kansas-alabama-georgia-north-carolina-ohio-alaska-midterm-elections), the [Rev. Adam Hamilton](https://hamiltonforkansas.com/), but [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) entities are starting to [invest heavily](https://punchbowl.news/article/campaigns/slf-kansas-spending/) in support of the struggling incumbent.

In [Nebraska](https://www.nebraska.gov/), [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Pete Ricketts (T-NE)](https://www.ricketts.senate.gov/) is facing a challenge from independent [Dan Osborn](https://www.osbornforsenate.com/), and the race was deemed competitive enough to [warrant a presidential rally](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-nebraska-ricketts-senate). What’s more, the [Senate Leadership Fund](https://senateleadershipfund.org/) has also [found it necessary to start investing](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/15/gop-super-pac-jumps-into-nebraska-senate-race-with-14m-ad-buy) in the contest.

In [Mississippi](https://www.ms.gov/), [Scott Colom](https://scottcolom.com/), a [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) district attorney, is taking on incumbent [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Cindy Hyde-Smith (T-MS)](https://www.hydesmith.senate.gov/), and [The Washington Post reported](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/01/has-mississippi-long-dead-zone-democrats-been-revived-by-trump-voting-rights/) last week that [Colom](https://scottcolom.com/) is proving to be such a capable candidate that “top [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) officials and donors increasingly hopeful that he could pull off the inconceivable and use this year’s poor political environment for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) to win a [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) race that just a few months ago was nowhere to be found on the party’s 2026 plans.”

A week earlier, [The Wall Street Journal similarly reported](https://www.wsj.com/politics/elections/mississippi-election-democrats-fundraising-ec77cb8a?mod=hp_lead_pos10) that some party officials and donors have taken a keen interest in the “once-unthinkable” race, adding, “[Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) [Minority Leader](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/majority-minority-leaders.htm) [Chuck Schumer (D-NY)](https://www.schumer.senate.gov/) has told donors at fundraisers and dinners in recent months to consider putting money into [Mississippi](https://www.ms.gov/).”

It’s reached the point at which [Hyde-Smith](https://www.hydesmith.senate.gov/), instead of ignoring her rival, has started to attack him [with baseless smears](https://www.clarionledger.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/13/us-senator-cindy-hyde-smith-of-ms-suggests-scott-colom-supporters-may-have-had-role-in-shooting/91594856007/).

My point is not that [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) are likely to win all of these races. Rather, what matters is that these races are even a subject of conversation, which necessarily reflects just how challenging the electoral landscape has become for the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/).

A few weeks ago, [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [John Kennedy (T-LA)](https://www.kennedy.senate.gov/) told reporters he’d scheduled trips to [Kansas](https://portal.kansas.gov/), [Nebraska](https://www.nebraska.gov/) and [Iowa](https://www.iowa.gov/) to support his party’s [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) candidates.

“If you told me six months ago I was going to be having to spend my time in those three states, I wouldn’t have believed you,” [he said](https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/6097894-sen-kennedy-campaigns-red-states/). “Those oughta be fat guy layups for us.”

That they’re not speaks volumes.
