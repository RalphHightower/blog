## [Officials cite ‘incompetence’ for massive FBI cyber hacking attack](https://www.ms.now/news/fbi-hack-shinyhunters-breach-data)

The hackers expressed disbelief that such sensitive information was available to them so easily, an [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) agent told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

By
[Ken Dilanian](https://www.ms.now/author/ken-dilanian-2) and [Carol Leonnig](https://www.ms.now/author/carol-leonnig)

Oct. 3, 2026, 3:41 PM EDT

The hack of [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) computer systems exposed sensitive personal information about nearly every [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) employee, as well as thousands of local law-enforcement officials who worked on [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) task forces, six current and former [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) officials told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

[FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) officials and outside experts are calling the breach one of the worst counterintelligence disasters in modern history. They say it raises questions about whether negligence was a factor in allowing it to happen.

One [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) agent who works on cybersecurity matters chalked the breach up to “incompetence.”

“This was a vulnerability that supposedly they thought they patched, but managed to miss one of the ways to patch it,” the agent said in a text message, adding that the cause was “definitely incompetence.”

The sensitive employee information that was stolen “should never have been tied to the Internet, either. It belonged on our internal system and some dumbass moved it all” to an internet-facing system, the agent said.

The agent added that the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) obtained chats in which the hackers expressed disbelief that such sensitive information was available to them so easily.

[Google](https://about.google/)’s threat intelligence experts issued a public warning in June about the vulnerability the hackers exploited, and the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) took steps to shore up its systems, two cyber security experts told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/), speaking on the condition of anonymity to discuss a sensitive matter.

But the hackers found a way around the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) patch, they said.

“It’s the fault of whoever in the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) is responsible for our cyber security,” said the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) cyber security agent who blamed the breach on “incompetence.” The agent added, “But also whoever pushed that information from our internal system to the Internet.”

An [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) official told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) that the hack was the result of a vulnerability in software maintained by a third-party vendor. The official didn’t respond to questions about the allegations of [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) incompetence.

Cybersecurity experts say the hacking group, which calls itself [ShinyHunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShinyHunters?wprov=sfla1) — a reference to a game pursuing rare and valuable [Pokémon](https://www.pokemon.com/) characters — got in through the [FBIJobs.gov](https://.fbijobs.gov/) portal by exploiting a previously unknown vulnerability in [Oracle PeopleSoft](https://www.oracle.com/applications/peoplesoft/), the human resources software. The hackers did not penetrate the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/)’s core investigative network or any classified systems, the current and former officials said.

Nonetheless, the information they stole could be extremely damaging.

An internal [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) message obtained by [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) said that the data exposed included names, home addresses, cell phone numbers, [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) email addresses and employee ID numbers, in addition to personal identifying information for emergency contacts, including [Social Security](https://www.ssa.gov/) numbers and personal email addresses.

The hackers obtained that information for nearly every [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) employee and also for more than 8,000 state and local law enforcement officers who worked on [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) task forces, current and former officials said. The [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) official confirmed that task force officers were victims and said they were being notified.

[ShinyHunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShinyHunters?wprov=sfla1) released a sample of 5,000 records but has since said its group does not plan to release the full data set. Still, experts warn that foreign adversaries, including [China](https://www.gov.cn/) and [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/), are likely trying to steal it.

“Beyond causing significant reputational damage to the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/), the incident poses a longer-term national security threat to the United States,” researchers from the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://www.csis.org/) think tank said in an analysis released this week.

Experts say the breach harkens back to the 2015 hack by [Chinese](https://www.gov.cn/) intelligence agents of the [Office of Personnel Management](https://www.opm.gov/), an attack that yielded [China](https://www.gov.cn/) information on nearly every federal employee, including fingerprints and highly sensitive details from their security clearance applications.

The information obtained from the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) in theory would provide adversaries a roadmap about agents and analysts working on sensitive matters, and could offer opportunities for foreign intelligence officials to blackmail or recruit vulnerable [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) employees, experts say.

In the employee notifications, the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) said it would offer sessions titled “Public Disclosures and Mitigation Strategies,” adding: “These briefings provide proactive steps all personnel can take to minimize vulnerabilities.”

“The [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) continues to investigate the recent Cyber incident, which we have confirmed involved a failure of a platform managed by a third-party vendor,” an [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) spokesman said in a statement.
