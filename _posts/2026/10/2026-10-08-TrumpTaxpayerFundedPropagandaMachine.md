## [Trump’s propaganda is not a ‘public service announcement’](https://www.ms.now/opinion/white-house-midterms-ad-propaganda-psa)

_Government-funded ads praising [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) are the opposite of “educational.”_

By [Hayes Brown](https://www.ms.now/author/hayes-brown)

Sept. 29, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT

The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) would deeply appreciate it if people would stop referring to the [government-sponsored ads](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-white-house-taxpayer-funded-propaganda-ads-midterm-elections) that recently began airing around the country as “propaganda.” Yes, the three ads heavily feature some combination of [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and his supposed accomplishments, snippets of speeches and praise for his vision, and little to no new information. But [the White House argued in a press release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/09/presidential-public-service-announcements-are-nothing-new/) issued Friday that these are all merely “public service announcements” serving as “a reminder for Americans to love their country and know why it’s worth defending — at home, at the border, and abroad.”

State-sponsored media, with the goal of tying patriotism to the current leader and promoting him as necessary to the country’s success, sure does sound like propaganda.

> State-sponsored media, with the goal of tying patriotism to the current leader and promoting him as necessary to the country’s success, sure does sound like propaganda.

 If it is, the ads clearly violate [a provision added to every government spending bill](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/publicity_or_propaganda) since the 1950s: “No part of any appropriation … shall be used for publicity or propaganda purposes within the United States not heretofore authorized by the [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/).” This prohibition does not apply to legitimate information-sharing and educational materials, which the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) claims these ads are.

The [Government Accountability Office](https://www.gao.gov/) has previously said there are three ways that government-produced media can escape the propaganda accusations. Whether in video, print or online, these materials cannot be “self-aggrandizing,” purely partisan in nature or covert propaganda that misleads the public about its source. The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) argues that the ads clear that bar, because they (however imprecisely) are U.S. government-funded, while “[President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) is not on the ballot and there is no call to action.”

The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) then listed three examples from recent [administrations](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/), two of which focused on public health. The [Obama administration](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/) used ads to [inform Americans about open enrollment periods](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/obama-admin-to-blast-tv-social-media-ads-before-open-enrollment) for [Affordable Care Act](https://www.healthcare.gov/)-created health insurance markets and [available subsidies](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/obama-administration-will-advertise-healthcare-subsidies-to-combat-perceptions-of-unaffordability-of-aca-health-plans/), while the [Biden administration](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/) [placed television ads encouraging vaccination](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2021/04/01/biden-pro-vaccine-tv-ads/) against Covid-19.

It seems that the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) did not vet its other modern example all that thoroughly. The [George W. Bush administration](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/) produced, among other materials, a set of print and television ads promoting recently enacted provisions of [Medicare](https://www.medicare.gov/) Part D coverage. The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [provides a link to a March 10, 2004, ruling](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-302504#:~) from the [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/) saying that the propaganda law hadn’t been violated.

But less than two months after that ruling, the [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/) learned that the [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services](https://www.cms.gov/) had not been upfront about who created a set of “video news releases” it had provided to news networks. The materials, which featured contractors posing as reporters in a news segment, [prompted the GAO to rule](https://www.gao.gov/assets/b-302710.pdf) that “the use of appropriated funds for production and distribution of the story packages and suggested scripts violated the publicity or propaganda prohibition” included in the latest government funding bill. ([Bush Justice Department disagreed](https://www.justice.gov/file/145731-0/dl?) as to whether [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/) had violated the “covert propaganda” ban but otherwise agreed with the [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/)’s framework.)

Incredibly, for the final bit of evidence that the [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) ads are not propaganda, the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) chose to cite literal wartime propaganda:

It’s not just modern Administrations. [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Woodrow Wilson](https://www.woodrowwilson.org/)’s [Committee on Public Information](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-woodrow-wilsons-propaganda-machine-changed-american-journalism-180963082/) [used](https://wwichangedus.org/topics/selling-the-war/) posters, films, and speakers to rally the country. [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/)’s [Office of War Information](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-9182-consolidating-certain-war-information-functions-into-office-war) [produced](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/articles-and-features/marshall-and-the-office-of-war-information/) films, radio, and posters to promote the war effort.

The government materials meant to keep Americans on board with World Wars I and II predate the “publicity or propaganda” provision, which first appeared in 1952. And as the links the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) itself provided explain, those efforts were explicitly propagandistic. The [last of those links](https://www.marshallfoundation.org/articles-and-features/marshall-and-the-office-of-war-information/) even quotes Elmer Davis, head of the WWII-era Office of War Information, admitting as much: “The easiest way to inject a propaganda idea into most people’s minds is to let it go through the medium of an entertainment picture when they do not realize that they are being propagandized.”

The [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) didn’t follow Davis’ advice: the three ads that have aired as of Monday are as subtle as a brick to the face. As such, it’s hard to see how they don’t fall under the category of “self-aggrandizement” or “puffery,” which [the GAO has defined](https://www.justice.gov/file/145731-0/dl?) as “publicity of a nature tending to emphasize the importance of the agency or activity in question.” And while there are no explicit mention of the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) Party or the upcoming elections, the tone and tenor are anything but apolitical.

Whichever account those funds were drawn from amounts to an illegal use of money that [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) never condoned.

Beyond that, we have known since [The Atlantic first reported it](https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/2026/08/trump-midterms-ads-maga/688409/) in late August that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) wanted ads promoting his achievements to air during the midterms. But while [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s super PAC would have [ostensibly paid for the ads under discussion](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/27/us/politics/trump-midterm-election-republicans.html), making them no less self-promoting but acceptable under the law, the ones aired last week were very much not.

It’s well documented that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [never pays himself for anything](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-uses-donations-america-pac-pay-legal-bills-rcna51883) when someone else can foot the bill. But spending taxpayer funds for shameless self-promotion ahead of a crucial election is beyond the pale even for him.

There’s been no indication of exactly which part of the government paid the ad makers that cut together these clips. For all we know, they may have been produced entirely in-house by government employees working on government-issued computers. Whichever account those funds were drawn from amounts to an illegal use of money that [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) never condoned. It is a scandal that would have rightly collapsed past [administrations](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/). It is to this era’s discredit that it is yet another day in the age of corruption [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has ushered into place.
