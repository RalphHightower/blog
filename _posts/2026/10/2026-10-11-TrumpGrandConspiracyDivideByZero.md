## [Opinion / The biggest problem with Trump’s ‘grand conspiracy’](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trumps-grand-conspiracy-fbi-doj)

_It turns out that facts matter, especially when trying to transform [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s revenge fantasies into a compelling legal case._

By [Hayes Brown](https://www.ms.now/author/hayes-brown)

Oct. 11, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT

The existence of a secretive group of enemies plotting against [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) since his first presidential campaign and the subsequent [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/) investigation, on through [his 2020 election loss](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-administration-2020-election-denialism) and many criminal indictments, has been an article of faith for the [MAGA](https://www.magapac.com/) movement. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s [fixation on gaining revenge](https://www.ms.now/opinion/msnbc-opinion/trump-doj-comey-james-bolton-indictments-rcna238195) against this supposed cabal of [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) officials and their so-called “deep state” allies lurks behind many of the biggest changes to the [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/) and FBI in his second term. For months, federal prosecutors and investigators have been tasked with cracking the “grand conspiracy case,” which would tie together a set of disparate threads that all lead back to [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s need for vengeance.

But as [The New York Times reported Friday](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/09/us/politics/trump-grand-conspiracy.html?smid=nytcore-ios-share) Friday, an array of factors has stymied those efforts. The largest issue, as the [Times](https://www.nytimes.com/) put it, is that the people working the case “have found it difficult, and time-consuming, to meet the standards of evidence and jurisdiction needed to prove anything close to a conspiracy in a court of law.” And everyone who touches the “grand conspiracy” case is forced to keep running headfirst into walls with little to show for it or be shown the door.

> Everyone who touches the “grand conspiracy” case is forced to keep running headfirst into walls with little to show for it or be shown the door.

The [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/)’s rush to find facts that might support [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s dark fantasy has led the [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/) to invert the usual rules of letting conclusions follow from evidence. People who disagree with this method have been pushed to the side. The [Times](https://www.nytimes.com/) [described](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/09/us/politics/trump-grand-conspiracy.html?smid=nytcore-ios-share) [Maria Medetis Long](https://www.floridabar.org/directories/find-mbr/profile/?num=1012329) as “a respected national security prosecutor in [Miami](https://www.miami.gov/), who approached the inquiry with an open mind.” But when she concluded that the evidence didn’t support [prosecuting former CIA Director John Brennan](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/08/26/us/politics/justice-department-investigation-fbi-russia-obama.html) for his role in helping open the [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/) probe, she was [removed from the case](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/17/us/justice-dept-trump-investigation-cia-brennan.html).

An old [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) ally, [former federal prosecutor Joseph diGenova](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/18/us/politics/digenova-trump-lawyer-conspiracy.html), was tapped to lead the [Southern District of Florida](https://www.flsd.uscourts.gov/)-based investigation instead. But last month, a reported difference in strategy saw [DiGenova](https://my.dcbar.org/directorymemberships?id=0014z00001ka8d6AAA) quit that role before [Attorney General](https://www.justice.gov/) [Todd Blanche](https://www.justice.gov/ag/staff-profile/meet-attorney-general) could fire him, as he believed was coming. According to [the Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/09/us/politics/trump-grand-conspiracy.html?smid=nytcore-ios-share), [DiGenova](https://my.dcbar.org/directorymemberships?id=0014z00001ka8d6AAA), “who wanted to take aim at big-name targets, told one associate he believed that [Mr. Blanche](https://www.justice.gov/ag/staff-profile/meet-attorney-general) was less interested in proving the conspiracy to destroy the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) than in securing rapid box-checking indictments of low-level players to keep [Mr. Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) happy.” [Blanche](https://www.justice.gov/ag/staff-profile/meet-attorney-general) in turn reportedly thought the 81-year-old [DiGenova](https://my.dcbar.org/directorymemberships?id=0014z00001ka8d6AAA) was “too scattered, too combative and too slow” to be effective.

[DiGenova](https://my.dcbar.org/directorymemberships?id=0014z00001ka8d6AAA) also apparently blamed the lack of resources provided to him. It’s true that not many career lawyers were open to volunteering for the assignment, but plenty of federal staffers have been diverted from other areas to focus on the grand conspiracy case and its offshoots. [FBI Director Kash Patel](https://www.ms.now/opinion/v) has talked up the supposed conspiracy for over a year now, including on [Joe Rogan’s podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C81bFx8CSA8); he also has pulled [hundreds of agents into chasing phantom election fraud](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/02/fbi-georgia-fulton-county-election-investigation) in [Georgia](https://www.georgia.gov/), mulled possible prosecutions of [his predecessor, James Comey,](https://www.ms.now/deadline-white-house/deadline-legal-blog/james-comey-indicted-again-trump-doj) and pursued other disparate elements of this imagined plot.

Even some who might be open to the existence of a “grand conspiracy” have been bumped for not believing hard enough. One member of “a special squad assembled by [F.B.I.](https://www.fbi.gov/) leaders” was veteran agent [Jack Eckenrode](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jack-eckenrode-3447142a7/?isSelfProfile=false), who aided past special counsel probes of [Comey](https://www.fbi.gov/history/directors/james-b-comey) and [praised Patel when he was nominated](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/justice/3302642/former-fbi-agents-boost-kash-patel-high-stakes-hearing/). But according to the [Times](https://www.nytimes.com/), [Eckenrode](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jack-eckenrode-3447142a7/?isSelfProfile=false) “questioned a central premise of the investigation — that the court-approved search of \[[Mar-a-Lago](https://www.maralagoclub.com/) in 2022] was a politically motivated intrusion that tainted everything that followed.” He reportedly pointed out that [U.S. District](https://www.flsd.uscourts.gov/) [Judge Aileen Cannon](https://www.flsd.uscourts.gov/content/judge-aileen-m-cannon), who has generally been a [been a boon for Trump’s legal fortunes](https://www.ms.now/opinion/msnbc-opinion/judge-cannon-trump-classified-documents-jack-smith-rcna161887), had already [ruled that the search was legal](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2024/06/25/trump-cannon-florida-hearing-evidence-affidavit-search/) making it hard to use it as evidence of a conspiracy. [Eckenrode](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jack-eckenrode-3447142a7/?isSelfProfile=false) was subsequently pulled from the case.

> What would make [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) happiest is to be the most special boy in the world, subject of a vast government-wide effort to take him down.

While the barrage of flimsy charges and half-baked probes has been [damaging to their targets](https://www.ms.now/opinion/david-hearn-reflecting-pool-trump-pirro), the leaders of [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s hunt are little better off than when they started. The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has already made an [example of his first attorney general, Pam Bondi,](https://www.ms.now/opinion/pam-bondi-doj-fired-trump-legacy), for failing to deliver on her assignment. [Blanche](https://www.justice.gov/ag/staff-profile/meet-attorney-general) and [Patel](https://www.fbi.gov/about/leadership-and-structure/director-patel) have been [doing their best to avoid similar](https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/2026/10/kash-patel-fbi-trump-midterm-elections/688805/). But much like software being told to divide by zero, they are no closer to achieving the impossible.

Proving the existence of a grand conspiracy is only necessary because it would vindicate [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s victimhood. What would make him happiest is to be the most special boy in the world, subject of a vast government-wide effort to take him down. The reality is much more mundane: [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) is a narcissistic, greedy conman who feels his greatest achievement — winning the presidency — was never fully celebrated. In the face of accusations of helping [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/) steal the [2016 election](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections2020.pdf) on his behalf, he proceeded to clumsily launch multiple botched efforts to rig the [2020 election](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections2020.pdf).

There can be no proof of a conspiracy that never existed and whose alleged members were merely reacting to [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s own villainy. The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), the wellspring of his own ills, would rather craft an elaborate fantasy than admit his own faults. The emperor has no case but still expects his followers to stand before a judge and point out his robes’ finest details. And there can be no happy ending for any of them until the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) sees his enemies in handcuffs.

[Hayes Brown](https://www.ms.now/author/hayes-brown) is a writer and editor for MS NOW. He focuses on policymaking at the federal level, including Congress and the White House.
