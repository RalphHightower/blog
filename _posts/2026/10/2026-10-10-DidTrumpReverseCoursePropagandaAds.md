## [Why Trump unexpectedly reversed course on taxpayer-funded campaign ads](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-campaign-ads-super-pac-taxpayer-money-election-midterms-propaganda)

_The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) is taking a step toward resolving an ongoing election-season scandal, but there are plenty of unanswered questions that need answers._

By [Steve Benen](https://www.ms.now/author/steve-benen)

Oct. 6, 2026, 8:58 AM EDT

As this week got underway and [JD Vance](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jd-vance-770a9047/) prepared to hit the campaign trail in [Alaska](https://alaska.gov/), a reporter asked the [vice president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/) a straightforward question: Does he believe publicly funded campaign ads are “a good use of taxpayer dollars”?

[The Ohio Republican replied](https://newrepublic.com/post/216204/jd-vance-donald-trump-ads-not-partisan), “I’m sure that some people don’t like it, but if you look at the message of those advertisements, they’re not partisan.” He added that, as far as he’s concerned, [there’s nothing “unusual”](https://bsky.app/profile/thebulwark.com/post/3mx5ckaxxw32f) about the election-season tactics.

Like too much of what the [vice president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/) says, the argument was plainly absurd. The [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has used taxpayer money [to air political propaganda](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-propaganda-taxpayer-funds-election-campaign-ads-midterms) — including one commercial that was one of [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s 2024 campaign ads — which is both legally dubious and unprecedented, making [Vance](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jd-vance-770a9047/)’s defense literally unbelievable.

But in an unexpected move, hours later, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) went in the opposite direction. [MS NOW reported](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-says-hell-personally-pay-for-taxpayer-funded-ads-after-bipartisan-backlash):

> [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s super PAC will pay for his campaign-style television ads moving forward, a [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) official told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) on Monday, shifting the cost away from taxpayers after the government-funded ad drew bipartisan backlash.

The clarification came hours after [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) said in a [Liars Club](https://truthsocial.com%?/) post that he would stop using taxpayer dollars to fund the ads and suggested he could pay for them himself or with money raised by [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) Asked whether [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) intended to use his personal money or his super PAC funds, a [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) official told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) that [MAGA Inc.](https://www.magapac.com/) will pay for the ads “moving forward.”

[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s [online statement](https://www.trumpstruth.org/statuses/42171) said he decided to reverse course in response to fury from “the Radical Left,” which was bizarre, not only because many [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) officials [publicly denounced the misuse of public funds](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-campaign-ads-propaganda-taxpayer-republicans-bipartisan-criticisms), but also because the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has never taken much of an interest in what has sparked complaints from “the Radical Left.”

What’s more, the fact that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s super PAC will pay for the ads “moving forward” leaves lingering questions about when, if ever, the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) intends to repay taxpayers for [the millions of dollars](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/02/us/politics/trump-ads.html) that have already been spent on the propagandistic messaging.

But as the dust settles on the latest developments, it’s also worth pausing to ask why, exactly, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) suddenly decided to abandon his plans. This obviously requires some speculation, but there are plenty of possibilities.

Maybe [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) lawyers told him this was a fight he’d lose: It is illegal under federal law to use public funds for “propaganda purposes,” and the [Government Accountability Office](https://www.gao.gov/) has said the prohibition applies to communications that are “purely partisan” and/or “designed to aid a political party or candidates.”

With this in mind, it’s very easy to believe the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) counsel’s office let [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and his team know that they were inviting real consequences — including possible impeachment efforts — by brazenly flouting legal limits.

Maybe the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) saw the polls: The latest [Reuters/Ipsos poll](https://www.reuters.com/world/americans-deeply-skeptical-taxpayer-funded-election-ads-reutersipsos-poll-finds-2026-10-06/) found that a whopping 86% of Americans — including 4 out of 5 self-identified [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) voters — agree that it’s wrong to use taxpayer money to pay for campaign ads.

With four weeks remaining before Election Day, it’s plausible to think [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) officials didn’t see much of a point to rallying behind a gambit opposed by 86% of the country.

Maybe [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) succumbed to [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) pressure: With so little time left in the cycle, it stands to reason that [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) officials and candidates asked the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) to change direction, rather than have this potentially illegal misuse of public funds detract from the party’s closing message.

Are we sure he’s actually reversing course? According to the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) and his team, they’re shifting the cost for the midterm ads away from taxpayers. The trouble is that the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) and his team say all sorts of things all the time, and many of those claims prove false.

If [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) really is going to stop misusing taxpayer money for this ad campaign, it’s a step toward resolving the scandal. But if recent history has taught us anything, it’s that the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s claims should be treated with reflexive skepticism, and it’ll be worth watching whether the cost burden for the campaign ads is actually shifted to [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s super PAC.
