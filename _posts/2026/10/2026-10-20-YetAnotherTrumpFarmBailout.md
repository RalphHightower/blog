## [White House approves new bailout for farmers hurt by Trump](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-bailout-farmers-diesel-costs-agriculture-midterm-elections)

_The one thing that all four of the [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/)’s bailouts have in common is that they’re effectively admissions of guilt._

By [Steve Benen](https://www.ms.now/author/steve-benen)

Oct. 20, 2026, 12:53 PM EDT

[Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s first bailout for farmers came [in 2018](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-administration-to-announce-12-billion-in-aid-to-farmers-affected-by-tariffs/), at which point the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) approved payments totaling \$12 billion. A year later, the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) [kept going](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/05/23/trump-to-give-16-billion-to-farmers-hurt-by-trade-war-sonny-perdue.html?msockid=17191dccdc2e67143e7c0adeddfa663f), announcing a bailout for farmers worth \$16 billion.

Late last year, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [launched another](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/the-top-10-problems-with-trumps-bailout-for-u-s-farmers) \$12 billion bailout for farmers, many of whom were harmed by the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s misguided and ill-fated trade tariffs policy, though the money proved insufficient. As my [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) colleague [Hayes Brown summarized](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-tariff-bailout-farmers), the policy was little more than “duct tape on a broken pipe,” which did “little to fix the underlying problem.”

In June, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) made it clear that he was [eyeing a possible fourth bailout](https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3mnl2mozocs2a) for the industry. And this week he followed through, [announcing an \$8 billion bailout](https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3mybhpfwadx2v) during a campaign rally in [Kansas](https://www.kansas.gov/), specifically aimed at helping farmers address higher diesel costs. The [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/) reported:

Under the plan, known as the [Harvest Fuel Relief Program](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/press-releases/2026/10/19/trump-administration-announces-8-billion-harvest-fuel-relief-program), the [Agriculture Department](https://www.usda.gov/) will use a formula based on how many acres and which crops farmers planted to reimburse them for a “portion” of their losses. The money will come from the [Commodity Credit Corporation](https://www.usda.gov/farming-and-ranching/resources-small-and-mid-sized-farmers/commodity-credit-corporation), a financing institution that the [Agriculture Department](https://www.usda.gov/) uses to fund crop insurance and natural disaster payment programs.

Payment rates will be announced next month, and farmers will receive money at a later date.

There’s ample reason to believe the \$8 billion will be insufficient. But just as notably, at no point during [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s event did he mention that farmers were struggling with higher diesel costs because of the war with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) that the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) launched eight months ago.

Indeed, the one thing that all four of the bailouts have in common is that they’re effectively admissions of guilt: [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s agenda continues to hurt his own country’s agriculture industry, which in turn has necessitated a series of payouts.

As for the timing of the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s latest announcement, it comes just two weeks before Election Day, with early voting underway in many states. It’s a safe bet that the bailout was intended to address intensifying discontent among voters in farming communities, who have long backed [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) but don’t appear to have much to show for it. [MS NOW reported](https://www.ms.now/opinion/rural-voters-farm-bankruptices-midterms) this week:

> After two decades of defeats in rural America, [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) are suddenly putting up a fight in farm country. The party is running competitive [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) races in [Iowa](https://www.iowa.gov/), [Kansas](https://www.kansas.gov/) and other farm states, as well as [House](https://www.house.gov/) races in heavily rural districts that could help it regain control of [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) in January.

Credit (or blame, depending on your party) for this reversal of fortune lies with [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s tariffs and his war with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), which have battered farmers and pushed the rural economy into crisis. The [Department of Agriculture](https://www.usda.gov/)’s analysts expect farmers’ net incomes to decline by 5.5% this year, as costs rise and receipts fall. The [National Agricultural Law Center](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/)’s tally of farm bankruptcies jumped 45% last year, and is on pace to rise another 11% in 2026.

Will [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s latest bailout affect voters’ attitudes, enough to stem the tide? We’ll find out in two weeks.
