## [FBI’s anti-corruption team eyed Susan Collins’ operation. Then Trump’s purge began.](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/susan-collins-fbi-navatek-navy-contract-corruption-allegations)

_With time running out ahead of Election Day, the [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/) [Republican](https://www.gop.com/)’s [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) rival said of the allegations, “This is corruption of the highest order.”_

By [Steve Benen](https://www.ms.now/author/steve-benen)

Sept. 22, 2026, 1:21 PM EDT

[Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Susan Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/)’ [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) critics have a handful of standard criticisms that have become staples of this year’s g
[Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) race in [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/). The longtime [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) incumbent, they argue, votes with [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s party the vast majority of the time despite her claim of being a “moderate”; she’s responsible for the consequences of some of her worst votes (including the confirmation vote on [Brett Kavanaugh](https://www.supremecourt.gov/)); and she’s changed after having been in Congress for far too long.

As a rule, accusing [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) of being involved in corruption hasn’t been part of the [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) script. It’s likely that will soon change.

[ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/) published [a rather devastating new report](https://www.propublica.org/article/fbi-susan-collins-navatek-campaign-donations-investigation) on the [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/) [Republican](https://www.gop.com/)’s political operation, which was worried about a possible defeat in 2020. To avoid that, Scott Reed, who led a [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/)-aligned [super PAC](), launched an aggressive fundraising initiative. From the article:

Reed was meeting that day with three executives from a [Hawaiian](https://www.hawaii.gov/) defense contractor, [Navatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Defense_Group?wprov=sfla1). A year earlier, [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) had helped their company land a multimillion-dollar [Navy](httpszero://www.navy.mil/) research contract in [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/). Now, seated at a coffee shop not far from the [U.S. Capitol](https://www.congress.gov/), Reed asked them for a \$500,000 donation.

Government contractors are banned from making political contributions. More consequentially, for the company to offer donations to [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) in exchange for an official action, or for [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) to accept, would constitute criminal bribery. But the company did have such a proposal: [Navatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Defense_Group?wprov=sfla1) was hungry for more government contracts in [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/). If they cut a big check, the CEO told Reed, [Navatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Defense_Group?wprov=sfla1) wanted [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) to guarantee tens of millions of dollars in additional federal funding.

According to [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/)’s reporting, which has not been independently verified by [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/), [Navatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Defense_Group?wprov=sfla1)’s CEO, [Martin Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1), planned to funnel the [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) donation through a shell company, and Reed assured him that the senator would understand where the money came from.

“Two months later,” the article added, Reed “told [Navatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Defense_Group?wprov=sfla1) executives that [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) committed to getting the company \$32 million in naval contracts, according to an internal company email reviewed by [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/).”

In 2022, the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) secured an indictment against [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1), accusing him of making illegal campaign contributions. [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1) came clean on all of the details surrounding the influence operation in the hope of getting a reduced sentence.

As recently as two years ago, [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) agents reportedly believed they had sufficient evidence to pursue what [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/) described as “a sweeping bribery probe that could ensnare top lawmakers of both political parties,” adding, “Although their effort was in its early stages, and it was unclear where it would lead, [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) agents asked [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1) extensive questions about his dealings with [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) and her office.”

So why did nothing happen? Because [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) returned to the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), and as part of his retaliatory revenge campaign, he and his team began to purge federal law enforcement, specifically targeting officials who had worked on cases the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) didn’t like.

The crusade left the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) effectively unable to pursue public corruption cases, and the leads generated by [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1)’s revelations withered on the vine.

The report quoted an [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) spokesperson saying the bureau had investigated claims against [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) “and ultimately found nothing implicating” the senator or her campaign. The senator’s office, meanwhile, “vigorously” denied allegations of wrongdoing and called [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1)’s claims “outlandish.” The same denial added that [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/)’ campaign was not part of the discussions between [Kao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kao?wprov=sfla1) and the [super PAC](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00698126/?cycle=2020), and her office had “fully cooperated” with the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) investigation.

[Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/)’ deputy chief of staff also posted a lengthy statement via social that said in part, “There have never been any allegations of wrongdoing by the [Collins for Senator](https://susancollins.com/) campaign, and the campaign was never the target of the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/) investigation.”

And yet, with just six weeks remaining in the midterm election cycle and polls showing [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) trailing in a tough re-election race, it’s a safe bet that voters in [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/) will be hearing a lot more about this very soon.

“This is corruption of the highest order,” [Troy Jackson](https://www.jacksonformaine.com/), the senator’s [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) rival, said in a statement on Tuesday morning. “[Susan Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) delivered millions in taxpayer dollars for a corrupt donor bankrolling her campaign, and then [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) helped her cover it up. No wonder she voted with him 96% of the time and has rubberstamped his agenda to hurt [Maine](https://www.maine.gov/).”

[Jackson](https://www.jacksonformaine.com/)’s statement concluded, “We deserve answers about every contract and every conversation that [Collins](https://www.collins.senate.gov/) and her team facilitated on the backs of taxpayers. The jig is up.”
