## [Trump hedges when asked about using the Insurrection Act ahead of midterms](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-insurrection-act-emergency-declaration-midterm-elections)

_In light of his track record, it’s tough to shrug when he dodges a question about possibly declaring a state of emergency ahead of the elections._

By [Steve Benen](https://www.ms.now/author/steve-benen)

Oct. 1, 2026, 10:06 AM EDT

[Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has never taken advantage of the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim), though the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has [talked about](https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3m2khkwqzgs2a) it [quite a bit](https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3lo2dfmszm32a) throughout his political career. A year ago this month, during a [Faux News](https://www.foxnews.com/) interview, the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [went so far as to say](https://bsky.app/profile/atrupar.com/post/3m3kjyarncn2b), “Don’t forget I can use the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim) … and that’s unquestioned power.”

Earlier this year, [he also said](https://newrepublic.com/article/205504/trump-insurrection-act-threat-courts) of the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim), “It does make life a lot easier. You don’t go through the court system.”

So when [Time](https://www.time.com/) magazine spoke with the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) for a lengthy, [newly published interview](https://time.com/article/2026/10/01/donald-trump-2026-interview-transcript/), it included a question about that unsettling possibility: “Would you rule out declaring a state of emergency in the lead up to the midterms unless there’s widespread violence in the streets?”

“I don’t rule anything out or in,” [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) replied. “We’ll see. You mean the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim)?”

When [Time](https://www.time.com/) clarified that it was, in fact, referring to the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim), the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) added, “No, I’ve never used it. I could use it. A lot of people think I should use it sometimes.”

At that point, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) changed the subject, talked about crime rates and [lied about how many](https://time.com/article/2026/10/01/donald-trump-2026-interview-fact-check/) undocumented immigrants entered the United States during [Joe Biden](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/)’s presidency.

The exchange came roughly a month after [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) spoke to a far-right conspiracy theorist [who pitched the president](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-root-national-security-election-midterms-constitutional-crisis) on the idea of unilaterally declaring a national security emergency for the midterm elections — even in the absence of an actual national security emergency — at which point [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) could theoretically impose wildly unnecessary voting restrictions on Americans by fiat and without congressional approval.

“Let me just say, stranger things have happened, OK?” [Trump said in response](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-root-national-security-election-midterms-constitutional-crisis) to the suggestion at the time. “I’ll leave it at that.”

To be sure, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) didn’t explicitly say he would invoke the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim) — a federal law that grants the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) emergency powers to deploy military troops on U.S. soil, who would act at his command — but when asked whether he would rule out declaring a state of emergency in the run-up to the midterms, the correct answer should have been, “Of course I’d rule that out.”

I can appreciate why these concerns might seem far-fetched, but there’s a pattern to consider. Earlier this year, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [mused publicly about canceling](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-midterm-elections-cancelling-2026) the midterm elections. A few months earlier, during a meeting with [Ukrainian President](https://president.gov.ua/) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](https://www.president.gov.ua/president/biografiya) — whose country cannot hold elections during [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/)’s deadly war — [Trump said](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-postpone-2028-election-zelenskyy-joke-dictator-rcna225893), “Say, three and a half years from now, so you mean, if we happen to be in a war with somebody, no more elections.”

There’s also the unavoidable fact that, after he lost his re-election bid six years ago, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) launched a radical and ultimately violent effort [to overturn the results he didn’t like](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/jan-6-whodunit-committee-finds-one-man-responsible-rcna63075), as part of a bid to remain in office despite the will of American voters.

So when [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) hedges after being asked about possibly declaring a state of emergency in the lead-up to the 2026 midterms, I find it difficult to shrug and laugh it off as meaningless nonsense.
