## [Trump era could be over following strong Democratic performance on Election Day](https://www.ms.now/news/news-analysis/donald-trump-2026-midterms-republican-losses)

_Political prognosticators are heralding the possible end of the [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) era._

By [Zack Stanton](https://www.ms.now/author/zachary-stanton)

Oct. 28, 2026, 12:06 PM EDT

2026 is showing us the future of American politics.

Tuesday could mark the end of the [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) era. That was [Nate Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/)’s read on [Ezra Klein’s podcast](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/27/opinion/ezra-klein-podcast-nate-silver.html).

“He’ll still be [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) for two more years,” [Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/) said. “But the minute the election, the votes, are counted … the 2028 election campaign begins, and if [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has a bad night, as polls show, then he’s a super-lame duck.”

[Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/) compared the political landscape to the [George W. Bush](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/) administration after 2006.

> [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) will “still be [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) for two more years,” [Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/) said. “But the minute the election, the votes, are counted … the 2028 election campaign begins, and if [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has a bad night, as polls show, then he’s a super-lame duck.”<br /><br />– [Nate Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/)

The map already looks like “a different regime,” [Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/) said. “[Kansas](https://www.ms.now/polling-kansas-senate) is competitive, [Texas](https://www.ms.now/polling-texas-senate) is competitive.” [Charlie Cook](https://www.cookpolitical.com/about/staff) called it “a unicorn election,” unlike any of the 22 he’s covered, [per the Cook Political Report](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/national/national-politics/unicorn-election-red-america). The swing group isn’t suburban independents but “normally reliable [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/),” many in farm country, who think [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) made “colossally bad decisions” on [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) and trade.

This cycle has also rewritten some of the rules for 2028.

For one, district maps are now redrawn mid-decade. [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) pushed [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) legislatures to redraw, and after the [Supreme Court](https://www.supremecourt.gov/)’s [Louisiana v. Callais ruling](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/608us1r29_n648.pdf), the South did just that.

The next redistricting round is already on the line Tuesday. [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) could win a majority of [governorships](https://www.ms.now/polling-governor) for the first time since 2010, and [Cook noted](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/governors/2026-final-governors-ratings) that states like [Georgia](https://georgia.gov/), [Ohio](https://ohio.gov/), [Nevada](https://www.nv.gov/) and [Iowa](https://www.iowa.gov/) “could be critical when it comes to upcoming redistricting fights and battles over election integrity in 2028.”

[Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) have moved on [Israel](https://www.gov.il/). A dozen [Biden](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/)-era officials who resigned over [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip?wprov=sfla1) released a pledge Wednesday morning asking 2028 candidates to reject both [Joe Biden](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/)’s and [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s [Israel](https://www.gov.il/) policies and not to hire the officials behind them, [per The Washington Sun](https://www.washingtonsun.com/foreign-policy/ex-biden-officials-want-2028-democrats-to-reject-biden-and-trump-israel-policies). Roughly half of [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) now say [Israel](https://www.gov.il/) committed genocide in [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip?wprov=sfla1). One [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) fundraising source told the [Sun](https://www.washingtonsun.com/) that 2028 campaigns were “waiting to see if [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) nominee] [Abdul \[El-Sayed\]](https://abdulforsenate.com/) wins” in [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/som) before deciding how much [Biden](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/)’s [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip?wprov=sfla1) record matters.

The attention economy has come to dominate politics. “Politics is the alchemy of turning attention into power,” [El-Sayed](https://abdulforsenate.com/) said, [per The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/27/us/politics/low-key-or-electric-the-style-gulf-splitting-the-democratic-party.html). [Vice President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/) [JD Vance](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jd-vance-770a9047/), a [MAGA](https://www.magapac.com/) standard-bearer in 2028, played the same game.

In a press gaggle Tuesday, [Vance](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/) called [El-Sayed](https://abdulforsenate.com/) “a bit of a psychopath” with “a lot of weird friends” and would not say whether he’s a jihadist, [per CBS News](https://x.com/JenniferJJacobs/status/2115096401520509352). Basically, the thinking is that conflict gets attention, and attention gets power. Not everyone is playing, though. [The Times](https://www.nytimes.com/) split [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) into two camps: “electric” and “normal,” and the normies are doing fine. “When was the last time you saw a viral clip from [Roy Cooper](https://roycooper.com/)?” the outlet asked. ([Cooper](https://roycooper.com/) is cruising in [North Carolina](https://www.nc.gov/).)

Heterodoxy is back for [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/). Wins by [Democrat](https://www.democrats.org/) [James Talarico](https://www.jamestalarico.com/) in [Texas](https://www.texas.gov/) or [Mary Peltola](https://www.marypeltola.com/) in [Alaska](https://alaska.gov/) would put pro-oil [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) from red states back in the [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/), where the current number is “a caucus of zero,” [per Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/28/the-midterms-may-revive-a-dying-breed-the-oil-state-democrat-01137632).

“All of the above is back, baby,” one [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) strategist said.

[Iowa](https://www.iowa.gov/)’s [Rob Sand](https://robsand.com/), who is running for [governor](https://governor.iowa.gov/), “eschews [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) norms,” opening town halls with a prayer and a rendition of “America the Beautiful.” He leads by 7 percentage points, according to [FifityPlusOne](https://fiftyplusone.news/) polling averages. In [Washington state](https://wa.gov/), the party is wide enough to include both [Reps.](https://www.house.gov/) [Pramila Jayapal](https://www.pramilaforcongress.com/) and [Marie Gluesenkamp Perez](https://marieforcongress.com/) — a former [Congressional Progressive Caucus](https://progressives.house.gov/) chief and a [Blue Dog Coalition](https://bluedogdems.com/) co-chair, respectively — who, despite very different records on transgender issues, have united to oppose a ballot measure forcing girls “to prove their sex to the state every year,” as [Gluesenkamp Perez](https://marieforcongress.com/) put it, [per MS NOW’s Julianne McShane](https://www.ms.now/news/midterm-elections-transgender-athletes-lgbtq).

Politics could be headed for a nationalists versus democratic socialists divide, à la [Europe](https://european-union.europa.eu/index_en). On the left, [Democratic Socialists of America](https://www.dsausa.org/) insurgents won high-profile primaries throughout the country, driven by youth enthusiasm, general anger at the [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) establishment and a sense that these are outsider candidates with normal, non-politico backgrounds.

The economic case is populist: A plurality of battleground voters say the biggest corruption problem is “greedy billionaires and corporations using their influence to rig the rules,” [per Cook](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/national/national-politics/voters-are-furious-about-corruption-what-does-corruption).

On the right, the closing message is about belonging.

“America belongs to those who love it,” [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) said in [Lubbock](https://www.mylubbock.us/), [Texas](https://www.texas.gov/), [per the Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/27/us/elections/paxton-talarico-texas-senate-rally-lubbock.html).

“[Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/som) belongs to those who love it, not those who hate it,” [Vance](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/jd-vance/) said in [Howell](https://www.cityofhowell.org/), [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/som), [per WDIV](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2026/10/27/vance-rallies-michigan-republicans-in-howell-ahead-of-november-election/).

[Vance](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jd-vance-770a9047/) also accused [El-Sayed](https://abdulforsenate.com/) of “explicitly anti-white racial hatred,” and at the rally said he “allegedly came from [Egypt]().” ([El-Sayed](https://abdulforsenate.com/) was, in fact, born in [Oakland County](https://www.oaklandcountymi.gov/), [Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/som), where [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney?wprov=sfla1) grew up.)

Politics is increasingly nationalized. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s gravity pulls every race toward him.

“Just close your eyes and pretend I’m on the ballot,” he has told crowds, [per The Associated Press](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/28/trump-midterms-himself-gop-congress/564194e8-d284-11f1-9931-2a41b76fc976_story.html).

Even [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [Sen. Susan Collins](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/susan-collins-maine-senate-election-82b52aff) called this the hardest race of her career. And the lack of local media outlets (213 counties have no local news source left, and 1,524 more have only one, [per Northwestern University’s Medill School of Journalism](https://www.medill.northwestern.edu/news/2025/news-deserts-hit-new-high-and-50-million-have-limited-access-to-local-news-study-finds.html)) means that more and more, politics is presented and consumed through a national prism. (Which is another reason it’s important that pols compete in the attention economy.)

Artificial intelligence is disrupting everything. [AI-manipulated videos](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-election-disinformation-russia-talarico-brown-c52f2c1720a0bb1ea86545d260aaf969), likely from the [Russian](http://kremlin.ru/) network Storm-1516, are spreading fake allegations about [Ohio](https://ohio.gov/) [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) nominee [Sherrod Brown](https://www.sherrodbrown.com/) and [Talarico](https://www.jamestalarico.com/).

And a [Princeton](https://www.princeton.edu/) researcher used roughly $20 in AI credits to reconstruct the order of nearly all in-person [ballots](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/28/election-security-paper-ballots-audits) in the [Georgia](https://georgia.gov/) counties he studied, and he could match some voters to their ballots. Data centers have become a closing message from [Wisconsin](https://www.wisconsin.gov/)’s [3rd Congressional District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin%27s_3rd_congressional_district?wprov=sfla1) to the [Kansas](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/28/democrats-rural-midwest-rebecca-cooke-01137050) and [Texas](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/governors/2026-final-governors-ratings) governor’s races. [Silver](https://www.natesilver.net/) thinks AI “could be the biggest issue of 2028.”
