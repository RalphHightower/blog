## [Trump is trying to distract from how Iran is only growing stronger](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-iran-air-force-one-turkey-escape-depleted-munitions)

[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s narrative of of total control over [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) is a fiction.

Aug. 14, 2026, 12:03 PM EDT
By [Zeeshan Aleem](https://www.ms.now/author/zeeshan-aleem)

[President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) thumped his chest about his [purported dominance of Iran](https://www.ms.now/news/iran-says-u-s-must-meet-all-conditions-before-strait-of-hormuz-can-reopen) on Wednesday, boasting on Truth Social that “the U.S.A. has total control over the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1),” and that “there is nothing [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) can do about it.” That’s because, he claimed, “they have no Navy, they have no Air Force, their remaining soldiers are unpaid, the [IRGC](https://sepahnews.ir/) is decimated and fleeing, and their ‘Leadership’ is uncertain, at best!”

You may not be surprised to hear that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s swaggering declaration is nonsense. Maritime traffic through the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1) [remains a fraction](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/13/middleeast/who-controls-strait-of-hormuz-iran-us-intl-hnk) of what it was before the war precisely because the U.S. cannot [guarantee safe passage](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-strait-of-hormuz-toll-plan-iran-war) for merchant vessels, and [Iran](https://irangov.ir/)’s missiles, drones and boats are still a threat.

[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) might have thought it was particularly important to peddle the fiction that he controls the war in light of news this week that [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) is looking as strong as it ever has.

> **[Iran](https://irangov.ir/) seems to be mastering asymmetrical warfare.**

After attending a [NATO](https://www.nato.int/) [summit](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/event-programmes/2026/07/nato-summit-2026?selectedLocale=) in [Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/) in July, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) reportedly covertly [sneaked off a legacy Air Force One aircraft](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-air-force-one-switch-deception-iran-threats) and flew out instead in an alternate military aircraft due to a reported [Iranian](https://irangov.ir/) assassination threat. Notably, journalists and some [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) staff members who boarded the plane were not notified that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) had been removed and effectively flew out on a decoy plane, [according to The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/08/10/trump-flew-secrecy-amid-iran-threat-air-force-one-became-decoy/).

It isn’t unprecedented for [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)s to fly in alternate aircraft or for the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s location to be kept secret in light of an emergency. But the [Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/) notes that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s decision to deceive the public about his location — and not disclose it afterward — highlighted “the depth of concern that U.S. security officials had about his safety leaving [Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/) amid hostilities with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/).” This elaborate move clearly contradicts [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s narrative that [Iran](https://irangov.ir/)’s capabilities have been obliterated. And his declining to inform the public is likely precisely because he knew the whole maneuver made him look weak, and [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) fearsome.

There’s increasing evidence that [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) is learning how to wear down U.S. munitions and break through defenses of U.S. bases in the Middle East. [The New York Times published](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/11/us/politics/iran-military-base-attacks-us-munitions.html) a report this week describing how [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) in July targeted three U.S. bases in [Jordan](https://kingabdullah.jo/) for five days using advanced missiles. While almost all of the missiles were shot down, at least one broke through. Three U.S. soldiers were killed, more than 100 were injured, and several aircraft were damaged.

The cost isn’t just to U.S. life but also its arsenal, the Times reported:

> _The [Pentagon](https://www.defense.gov/) has used more than 1,500 Patriot interceptor missiles in the war and has fewer than 1,700 interceptors left, according to two people briefed on the stockpile levels who spoke on the condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military details. The United States produced about 600 interceptors in all of 2025._

Worth noting: Each interceptor missile costs about \$4 million and takes more than two [two years to make](https://www.wsj.com/world/why-does-it-take-years-to-get-a-patriot-missile-from-factory-to-front-line-3e5874c5).

The U.S. has also [about a quarter](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/08/13/us-military-has-lost-roughly-25-its-reaper-drones-iran-war-depletes-arsenal/) of its Reaper drone fleet during the war. Each one costs between \$30 million and \$50 million.

Between its ability to effectively shutter the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1) and to force the U.S. to burn through its high-cost advanced weaponry, [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) seems to be mastering asymmetrical warfare.

That [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) keeps [backing down](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/03/world/middleeast/iran-trump-escalation.html) after threats to dramatically escalate the war against [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) shows a recognition of that reality. He just doesn’t want to admit it to the public. But that doesn’t mean that the public hasn’t caught on.
