## [Jim Jordan's criminal referral of Jack Smith has a glaring flaw](https://www.ms.now/opinion/jim-jordan-jack-smith-justice-department-criminal-charges)

_The [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) [congressman](https://jordan.house.gov/) has accused [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) of lying because the former special counsel did not answer a question he was not asked._

Jul. 28, 2026, 3:13 PM EDT
By [Duncan Levin](https://www.ms.now/author/duncan-levin)

[Rep.](https://www.house.gov/) [Jim Jordan](https://jordan.house.gov/) wants the [Justice Department to investigate former special counsel Jack Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) for lying to [Congress](https://www.ms.now/news/jim-jordan-criminal-referral-jack-smith). Those of us who served with [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) as federal prosecutors know this accusation is almost farcical. For those who do not know him personally, a close reading of the testimony at issue makes clear this is less a good-faith dispute over something [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) told the [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Judiciary Committee](https://www.house.gov/committees) than an effort to manufacture a crime from a truthful answer.

In the U.S. [Attorney’s Office](https://www.justice.gov=) for the [Eastern District of New York](https://www.justice.gov/edva/), [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) was known as an exacting, direct and intensely evidence-driven prosecutor. He was not given to verbal gamesmanship, nor did he have a casual relationship with the truth. That history does not place him above scrutiny, but it discredits the portrait underlying [Jordan’s referral](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/22/us/politics/jack-smith-jim-jordan-charges.html). The facts make it less credible still.

> **A close reading of the testimony at issue makes clear this is less a good-faith dispute over something [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) told the [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Judiciary Committee](https://www.house.gov/committees) than an effort to manufacture a crime from a truthful answer.**

The dispute concerns [Smith’s testimony in late 2025 about records obtained during the special counsel’s investigation](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/rep-jim-jordan-refers-jack-smith-for-possible-prosecution-saying-he-have-have-misled-on-trump-probe) of [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s efforts to overturn the [2020 election](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections2020.pdf). [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) was asked in [closed-door testimony](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/jim-jordan-jack-smith-testimony-hidden-public)  — which he had previously [asked be open to the public](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/jack-smith-ready-respond-gop-questions-wants-public-see-answers-rcna239538) — whether subpoenaed telephone toll records associated with members of [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) included the contents of lawmakers’ phone calls or text messages.

Toll records are metadata. They show which numbers communicated and the time and duration of calls. They do not reveal what was said or written. When [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) was asked whether the subpoenaed toll records included the contents of phone calls or text messages, he answered no. That answer was accurate: The subpoenas sought metadata, not the contents of the communications.

Separately, investigators with [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith)’s team obtained presidential records from the [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/). Those presidential records included some text messages exchanged between members of [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) and [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) officials. [Jordan](https://jordan.house.gov/) argues that [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith)’s testimony about the toll-record subpoenas gave the false impression that his team never had access to the contents of lawmakers’ messages, because [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) did not volunteer that messages involving lawmakers appeared in a separate collection of presidential records.

That’s it. That is the alleged contradiction. Separate sets of records came from different sources and were obtained through different legal processes. [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) answered a question specifically about one set of records without offering information about other records that were not mentioned in the question.

Had lawmakers wanted to know whether investigators obtained congressional communications from any source, [Judiciary Committee](https://www.house.gov/committees) members could have asked that. They did not. The committee is now rewriting its question after the fact and accusing [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) of lying because he did not answer a question he was not asked.

Federal law on false statements requires an actually false statement, made knowingly and willfully. It does not criminalize a witness’s failure to volunteer related facts a congressional questioner later decides should have been included. If a truthful answer could be converted into a felony simply by enlarging the question months after testimony is given, then truthfulness itself offers no protection.

There may be legitimate oversight questions about how [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith)’s team handled communications involving members of [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/). The [Constitution](https://constitution.congress.gov/)’s speech-or-debate clause protects legislative activity from executive branch intrusion. [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) has every right to examine whether investigators observed the appropriate constitutional safeguards. But [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) does not vindicate those concerns by pretending that an accurate answer about toll records was a lie about presidential records. If lawmakers believe the investigation exceeded its authority, they should make that argument — honestly.

Context makes the purpose of [Jordan](https://jordan.house.gov/)’s referral hard to miss. [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) brought two federal criminal cases against [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/). [He ended both after Trump was re-elected in 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/11/25/us/politics/jack-smith-trump-election-documents-charges.html) out of recognition of the [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/)’s long-standing position that a sitting president cannot be federally indicted or criminally prosecuted. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s re-election did not answer the allegations, test the evidence or vindicate him in court. It simply restored the one thing no criminal defendant is supposed to possess: control over the machinery of federal prosecution.

Now that machinery is being pointed against [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith).

Special prosecutors exist because of the decades-old recognition that an administration cannot credibly supervise an investigation of the president or his closest associates. During [Watergate](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/american_originals/nixon.html), [President Richard Nixon](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/) ordered the firing of special prosecutor Archibald Cox after Cox insisted on obtaining [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) recordings. The [Saturday Night Massacre](https://www.npr.org/2018/10/21/659279158/a-brief-history-of-nixons-saturday-night-massacre) became infamous because the public understood what had happened: A president under investigation had sought to wield his power against the prosecutor investigating him, and the attorney general and deputy attorney general resigned rather than carry out [Nixon](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/)’s order.

Later, the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) former federal judge appointed to investigate the [Iran-Contra affair](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/archives/audiovisual/white-house-photo-collection-galleries/iran-contra), Lawrence Walsh, described the underlying principle plainly: When the attorney general cannot represent the public rather than the president, he wrote, “[the rule of law requires that another, independent institution assume that responsibility](https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/part_xi.htm).”

Today, [Jordan](https://jordan.house.gov/)’s referral turns that principle upside down.

Political allies have scoured [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith)’s testimony for an omission, called the omission a lie and asked the president’s [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/) to treat it as a crime.

Prosecutors exercise enormous power and must be held accountable if they break the law. But a legitimate investigation starts with evidence of a crime and follows that evidence to the person responsible. Starting with the person to be punished and working backward until something can be made to resemble a crime is political retaliation.

Every prosecutor handling a politically sensitive matter will understand the lesson. Investigate someone powerful enough, and the legal system you served may later be redirected against you. Your testimony can be removed from context, an accurate answer reframed as an omission and the omission presented as criminal intent.

[Jordan](https://jordan.house.gov/)’s referral need not produce an indictment to inflict significant damage. Investigation, public accusation and years of legal expense can punish someone government prosecutors never had evidence to convict. And the spectacle tells future prosecutors that following evidence toward political power may eventually place their own freedom and reputation at risk.

The [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/) should read the question [Smith](https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco-smith) was asked and the answer he gave. This is political revenge made to look like law enforcement.
