## [The simple reason high gas prices could cost Republicans in November](https://www.ms.now/news/high-gas-prices-republicans)

_The combination of a midterm election and high gas prices could be difficult for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) to overcome — but there’s good reason to believe it could actually be worse this year for the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/)._

Aug. 17, 2026, 5:00 AM EDT
By Lillie Boudreaux

Experts call it “The Iron Law”: The sitting [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s party almost always loses [House](https://www.house.gov/) seats during the midterm election. But that tendency can be exacerbated — or blunted — by a key factor: gas prices.

As [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) scramble to defend their [House](https://www.house.gov/) majority in November, gas prices are becoming a major concern — both for voters and for the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmakers trying to hold onto their seats.

A recent [Politico analysis](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/06/25/republicans-high-gas-prices-midterms-00974308) found that incumbents tend to pay a more significant electoral price during midterm elections when costs at the pump are higher, with the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s party losing 32 [House](https://www.house.gov/) seats on average since 1978 when gas costs more, and only shedding six seats when prices are down.

But for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/), that history is taking on new significance for a simple reason: [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) can be blamed for the spike in gas prices. There’s a direct line between the war [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) launched in Iran and the higher costs, and [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) have done little to rein in the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) or even call out the consequences of his decision.

The situation has put [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmakers in a political bind: they are stuck defending the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/)’s economy and [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com)’s handling of the [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) war while also showing constituents they understand and sympathize with the pain they’re feeling at the pump.

[Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) know it’s a difficult spot, and some [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmakers acknowledge that they and their party need to do a better job.

“The bottom line is, really and truly, if you’re not concerned and sympathetic about gas prices for people, then really and truly, we’re not thinking very right, because we got a lot of people that are really hurting,” [Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Jim Justice (T-WV)](https://www.justice.senate.gov/), told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

[Justice](https://www.justice.senate.gov/) added that, if [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) weren’t concerned by the rise in gas prices, “they’re living in a cave.”

“The political blowback will be substantial,” he said

[Sen.](https://www.senate.gov/) [Lisa Murkowski (T-AK)](https://www.murkowski.senate.gov/) argued the political fallout for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) is already here.

“I think we’re already seeing blowback from people, from Americans, about the affordability issue,” [Murkowski](https://www.murkowski.senate.gov/) told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/). “And that absolutely includes gas prices.”

Gas prices skyrocketed at the start of the [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) war, and have remained high, as the conflict continues to disrupt global trade routes. The national average for a gallon is above \$4, according to [AAA](https://gasprices.aaa.com/state-gas-price-averages/), and it’s roughly 30% higher than it was a year ago.

One swing-district [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) they are “just as frustrated” about the elevated gas prices as voters.

“I wish the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) had a plan,” this [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/). “Obviously the fuel prices are having a devastating impact on people across the district and across the country.”

This [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) added that the people most upset about gas prices were “ultimately the folks that we need to turn out.”

But [National Republican Congressional Committee Chair](https://www.nrcc.org/) [Rep.](https://www.house.gov/) [Richard Hudson (T-NC9)](https://hudson.house.gov/) — who is tasked with holding the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) majority in the [House](https://www.house.gov/) — has defended the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/)’s actions in [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) and put the responsibility on candidates to explain it to voters.

“[President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) did the right thing, and we need to support what he’s doing. And if that means we have to go to the voters and explain it, so be it,” [Hudson](https://hudson.house.gov/) told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) last month. “That’s our job.”

[Hudson](https://hudson.house.gov/) added that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) was “saving the world from a crazy ayatollah with a nuclear weapon in [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/),” and he tried to thread the needle on recognizing the economic pain that voters are feeling by arguing it’s temporary.

“As soon as we can resolve the situation in [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/), your gas prices are going to plummet,” he said.

[Hudson](https://hudson.house.gov/) pointed to gas prices under the [Biden administration](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/), which reached record highs in 2022 largely due to supply chain concerns following [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/)’s invasion of [Ukraine](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/). [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) lost nine seats in that midterm election and, subsequently, control of the chamber.

But this year is somewhat different. Drivers have endured elevated prices with little reprieve for months, and prices are at the highest level ever for this time of year. Unlike in 2022, when [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) could point to an external crisis and blame the incumbent party, [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) are also already tying today’s prices directly to [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com)’s decision to go to war with [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/).

And now [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) himself is making that argument easier for [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/).

The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has repeatedly acknowledged that the [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) war is contributing to higher energy costs — while arguing that Americans should accept the pain as the price of preventing [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) from obtaining a nuclear weapon.

On Friday, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) urged Americans to accept “a tiny little bit more” for gasoline as the cost of confronting [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/), saying that preventing the country from obtaining a nuclear weapon was worth the higher prices and that he would “never apologize” for that trade.

And in May, he said he doesn’t think about the “financial situation” of Americans.

“I don’t think about anybody,” [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) said. “I think about one thing: we cannot let [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/) have a nuclear weapon. That’s all. That’s the only thing.”

It all points to a tough climate for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/).

Dr. Ross Burkhart, a [political science professor](https://www.boisestate.edu/sps-politicalscience/) at [Boise State University](https://www.boisestate.edu/) who has studied the ties between gas prices and election outcomes, said his research shows a moderate correlation between party approval ratings and gas prices. But this November, he said, that correlation could be much stronger.

“This year, I think there is truly a clear connection between actions taken by [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com) and their impact on gas prices,” Burkhart said. “And I think this year thus is quite different from previous midterm elections in that regard.”

[Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) are seizing on the opportunity to appeal to voters.

“[Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) promised not to start endless wars in the Middle East, but to stop them, and then turned around and got us involved in this reckless and costly war of choice in [Iran](https://https://irangov.ir/), and gas prices are skyrocketing out of control in an economy that is suffocating and drowning the American people,” [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) leader [Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY6)](https://jeffries.house.gov/) said during a press conference last month.

Gas prices are a particular concern for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/), as their constituents drive 26% more miles than a [Democratic](https://www.democrats.org/) member’s average constituent, according to [The Brookings Institution](https://www.brookings.edu/).

But [Adie Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/), a senior fellow at [The Brookings Institution](https://www.brookings.edu/), told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) that greater concern is “more about geography than it is partisanship.”

“[Republican](https://www.gop.com/) members, their constituents right now are feeling it more so than [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/),” Tomer said.

“Gasoline is one of those unavoidable costs. It might as well be like food to most households, because over 95% of households drive,” [Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/) added. “So we know that elevated gasoline prices for sustained periods of time, which is exactly what we’re in right now, is going to be top of mind when they go to vote.”

And [Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/) said voters don’t have to pay much attention to the news to know “who drove the situation.”

“This is going to be really tough for incumbents, particularly those associated with the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), because it’s just going to be hard to get out from it,” [Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/) told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

The impact on people’s wallets is a key indicator for election forecasters like [Dr. Michael Lewis-Beck](https://politicalscience.uiowa.edu/people/michael-lewis-beck) and [Dr. Charles Tien](https://www.hunter.cuny.edu/people/charles-tien/), who developed a political economy model that considers [presidential](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) approval ratings and disposable personal income to estimate how many seats the incumbent will lose in November.

Their “referendum” model predicts that [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) will lose 33 [House](https://www.house.gov/) seats in the midterm election — which, as [Lewis-Beck](https://politicalscience.uiowa.edu/people/michael-lewis-beck) put it, would be “a serious blow to the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) Party.”

A [Politico poll](https://www.politico.com/f/?id=0000019f-9504-dd42-ab9f-d507a5600000) found that 46% of voters said gas prices will impact the way they plan to vote in November. But only 15% of those who said it will impact their vote said they would vote for a different party.

“People are very resistant to changing their party, even if the house is floating down the river from a flood,” [Lewis-Beck](https://politicalscience.uiowa.edu/people/michael-lewis-beck) said.

Mychael Schnell and Syedah Asghar contributed to this report.

Lillie Boudreaux is a desk associate at [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/). She primarily covers politics and Capitol Hill.
