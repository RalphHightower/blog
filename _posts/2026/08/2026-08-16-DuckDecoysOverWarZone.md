## [The biggest issues with Trump's Air Force One ruse aren’t the decoys](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-air-force-one-turkey-decoy)

_[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s plane switch in [Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/) illustrates three worrying issues that are bigger than the unsuspecting journalists and [Cabinet members](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/the-cabinet/)._

Aug. 14, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT
By [Kevin Baron](https://www.ms.now/author/kevin-baron)

I love my media colleagues, but they and others being made decoys in [President Donald Trump’s secret, switcheroo plane swap](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-air-force-one-switch-deception-iran-threats) in [Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/) last month so the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) could avoid [a perceived international assassination threat](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-plane-swap-air-force-one-catering-truck-turkey) is not the top story. We should focus on the first fact: that there was a perceived international assassination threat against the [U.S. president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) so serious that security officials believed he had to be pulled off [Air Force One](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104588/vc-25-air-force-one/), put on a separate plane and secreted away from a [NATO summit in Ankara](https://www.ms.now/news/trump-nato-summit-allies-ukraine-defense), the capital city of a member country.

That shouldn’t happen.

The escapade illustrates three worrying issues: First, whether an actual threat existed or not, there appear to be U.S. intelligence and security gaps. Second, animosity toward [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has reached a level that such a foreign attempt would seem plausible, especially in [Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/). Third, U.S. and [Israeli](https://www.gov.il/) intelligence services have credibility problems, which many believe are being caused by the countries’ top leaders.

> **The escapade illustrates three worrying issues: First, whether an actual threat existed or not, there appear to be U.S. intelligence and security gaps.**

Was the threat real? If not, then why did U.S. officials so readily believe it? According to multiple reports, [the warning did not come from U.S. intelligence](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/08/12/cia-had-low-confidence-iranian-threat-before-trump-switched-planes-turkey/); [Israeli intelligence alleged](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/12/g-s1-138354/trump-secret-flight-israel-iran-air-force-one) alleged there was a threat from an [Iranian](https://irangov.ir/)-sourced shoulder-fired missile. Such missiles are especially frightening because they are so hard to detect or defend. [CIA](https://www.cia.gov/) officials assessed the claim as “[Israeli](https://www.gov.il/)-derived, not U.S.-generated, and viewed as low confidence,” [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/) reported Wednesday. Those [CIA](https://www.cia.gov/) officials reportedly told [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) officials as much. The [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) was moved anyway.

As with any credible threat of [presidential](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) assassination, [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) should quickly hold classified and unclassified hearings with [Secret Service](https://www.secretservice.gov/), U.S. military and intelligence leaders. The questions should focus on what those involved knew before and during the summit, when they knew it and why the decision was made for [Air Force One](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104588/vc-25-air-force-one/) to take off without [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) but loaded with [Cabinet members](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/the-cabinet/), federal government staff, military personnel and, yes, journalists.

Unfortunately, [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) leaders in [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/) have been unwilling to challenge or undercut the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/). [Trump said Tuesday](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/08/12/trump-says-secret-service-told-him-switch-planes-because-threat/) he was just obeying the [Secret Service](https://www.secretservice.gov/) and military: “They wanted me to do it, so I do it. I do what they say.” But the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), [Pentagon](https://www.defense.gov/), and [State Department](https://www.state.gov/) have large advance teams that scout out every detail of foreign visits such as a [NATO](https://www.nato.int/) summit, and if this threat was real, then something was missed. If the threat wasn’t real, then that’s a problem, too. [The buck should stop somewhere](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-hegseth-pirro-blame).

I’ve never flown aboard an [Air Force One](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104588/vc-25-air-force-one/), but as a [Pentagon](https://www.defense.gov/) reporter in the traveling press corps, I know why reporters are expressing such shock and outrage. I spent what feels like half my life flying on its military twins, the [E4-B “doomsday planes”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=meIuEVhyROQ) used by the secretary of defense. While [Air Force One](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104588/vc-25-air-force-one/) is the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s elegant flying [Oval Office](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), the [E4-B](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104503/e-4b/) is the SecDef’s 1970s-era workhorse flying nuclear command center, complete with a five-mile-long cable to send secret launch orders to ballistic-missile submarines.

The farther we traveled from [Washington](https://dc.gov/), the more our traveling parties felt like traveling families. Reporters don’t wear the uniforms of the military leaders they cover, and government officials don’t hold their pens, but in those planes and on those trips, our lives were in each other’s hands. Every planned movement — from the time we left [Joint Base Andrews](https://www.jba.af.mil/) to each motorcade, helicopter ride and event — is protected for operational security, or “OPSEC.” Trust is how we survived. I can’t imagine landing in Washington to find out we were sent into the air as bait.

> **I can’t imagine landing in [Washington](https://dc.gov/) to find out we were sent into the air as bait.**

My experience covering the [Pentagon](https://www.defense.gov/) confirms the long-held truth that foreign intelligence, security and military professionals maintain close and trusting relationships despite the politics of their country’s leaders. But while [Israeli](https://www.gov.il/) intelligence historically is known to be among the world’s best, in recent years, the credibility of [Israel](https://www.gov.il/)’s security services under [Prime Minister](https://www.gov.il/en/departments/prime_ministers_office) [Benjamin Netanyahu](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/netanyahu-b) has, in the eyes of many, stretched thin or vanished. True or not, many critics believe the [United States](https://www.usa.gov/) launched a war on [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), not because there was an imminent threat but because, in [a phone call with Trump](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/03/trump-netanyahu-call-iran-war-israel-coordination), [Netanyahu](https://www.gov.il/en/pages/netanyahu-b) told [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/), “[Iran](https://irangov.ir/)’s supreme leader and his top advisers were all set to meet at one location.”

If the shoulder-fired missile threat was an [Israeli](https://www.gov.il/) ruse to dupe the [American president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) into continuing a war with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), and the U.S. can’t trust intelligence from its [Israeli](https://www.gov.il/) allies, then we have a problem much bigger than a catering truck.

[Turkey](https://global.tbmm.gov.tr/) may be the dupe in this drama. [NATO](https://www.nato.int/) deliberately held a summit in [Ankara](https://www.ankara.bel.tr/seffaflik-ve-hesapverebilirlik) to prove a point that it was safe enough to do so, yet [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) could not depart in his own plane. Is it wise to keep sending world leaders so close to [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) and [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/)?

From start to finish this incident reveals too many elevated risks threatening national security and the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s. As long as [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) continues to direct U.S. forces to attack [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), any [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)ial movement puts everyone traveling near him at greater risk. For now, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has said he expects to travel back to [China](https://www.gov.cn/) for an [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://www.apec.org/), or [APEC](https://www.apec.org/), meeting this fall and to visit his golf course in [Ireland](https://www.gov.ie/en/) in September. Those trips would be much safer for everyone aboard [Air Force One](https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104588/vc-25-air-force-one/) if he chose to end the war he started. Until then, security teams will have a harder time protecting them all.
