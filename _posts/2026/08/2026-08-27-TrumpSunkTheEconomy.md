## [From Canada to Iran, Trump can't stop sabotaging his own economy](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-canada-iran-trade-bessent-beef)

_[Presidents](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) usually get far too much credit or blame for the state of the economy. But in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s case, the causation is far clearer._

By [James Downie](https://www.ms.now/author/james-downie-2)
Aug. 26, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT

“Why would I want to blow up the global financial system?” an exasperated [Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/) [Secretary Scott Bessent](https://home.treasury.gov/about/general-information/officials/scott-bessent) said during a press conference Monday, while introducing [new sanctions against Iran](https://www.ms.now/news/bessent-iran-war-new-sanctions-economic-d-day). [Bessent](https://home.treasury.gov/about/general-information/officials/scott-bessent) may think that’s still a self-evidently ridiculous question. [George W. Bush](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/) didn’t want a [Great Financial Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis?wprov=sfla1), nor did [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover?wprov=sfla1) seek to start a [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression?wprov=sfla1). Surely no [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) would deliberately hurt the global economy.

Yet for the first time ever, that’s not the case. At home and abroad, the past few days have laid bare the extent to which [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s troubles on the economy are self-inflicted.

> **The [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1) is not open, oil prices are still high, and the knock-on effects are spreading beyond the gas pump.**

Last week, the yield on the 30-year Treasury bond [reached a 19-year high](https://www.ms.now/opinion/scott-bessent-treasury-deficit-bond-trump). The [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) took notice, perhaps because higher bond yields usually mean [higher borrowing costs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/18/mortgage-rates-treasury-bond-yields-consumer-loans.html) at a time when voters are already furious about the cost of living. [Bessent](https://home.treasury.gov/about/general-information/officials/scott-bessent)’s solution was to intervene in bond markets by [increasing the size of debt buybacks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/20/bond-yields-edge-higher-as-traders-digest-treasury-debt-buyback-plan.html). But yields dropped for only a day before returning to their previous level, reflecting investors’ broader structural fears, including over the \$40 trillion national debt.

For that, the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) can thank its own tax cuts, as well as those passed under the first [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/). As [Bobby Kogan](https://www.americanprogress.org/people/bobby-kogan/) of the [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/) [wrote for MS NOW Tuesday](https://www.ms.now/opinion/federal-debt-40-trillion-trump-tax-cuts), “it is a mathematical truth that had the [\[George W.\] Bush](https://georgewbush-whitehouse.archives.gov/index.html) and [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) tax cuts never been enacted, the U.S. debt ratio would be declining indefinitely.” Even if the deficit-busting impact of the 2017 tax cuts somehow came as a surprise to the first [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/), there was no way to expect any other outcome for last year’s “big, beautiful bill.”

Two days after [Bessent](https://home.treasury.gov/about/general-information/officials/scott-bessent)’s bond intervention, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) announced he will allow the [allow the import of 300,000 tons of ground beef](https://www.ms.now/news/republican-lawmakers-congress-trump-beef) with lower tariffs. Though he did not specify which companies or countries will provide the beef, the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) promised it would “substantially lower the price of ground beef” just before the midterm elections. In other words, he admitted that U.S. consumers have been paying the costs of his tariffs after months of insisting that other countries would foot the bill. In the meantime, his failed, and in some cases illegal, trade policies [cost Americans billions](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/24/nx-s1-5939518/u-s-businesses-are-getting-tariff-refunds-will-customers-get-any-money-back).

At least on these two issues, the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) is attempting to mitigate the damage. The same cannot be said of [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s approach to two economic conflicts.

> **[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/), like many a businessman turned politician, has long presented himself as someone who gets stuff done.**

The human toll of the [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) war is terrible enough: tens of thousands of [Iranians](https://irangov.ir/) and nearly 800 U.S. service members [killed or wounded](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2026-08-21/iran-war-casualty-increase-22617896.html). With [the president bored](https://www.theatlantic.com/national-security/2026/05/iran-war-trump-deal/687100/) and frustrated at being unable to bomb his way to peace, the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) is betting on new sanctions – and [threats of new sanctions](https://www.ms.now/news/bessent-iran-war-new-sanctions-economic-d-day) against other countries – to secure [Tehran](https://tehran.ir/)’s capitulation. But the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1) is not open, oil prices are still high, and the knock-on effects are spreading beyond the gas pump. “Companies say they are raising prices on products ranging from french fries and beer to paint and packaging to offset their own rising commodity and freight costs,” [the Wall Street Journal reported last month](https://www.wsj.com/business/iran-war-pushes-companies-to-raise-prices-on-beer-paint-fries-and-more-e55d31b2).

At the same time, the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) has restarted its trade war with [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/). The two countries announced billions in new tariffs, which will make products more expensive on both sides of the border. Though the U.S. may be far larger, as economist [Paul Krugman wrote](https://paulkrugman.substack.com/p/the-moose-that-roared?r=2203u&), “the U.S. economy is far more dependent on [Canadian](https://www.canada.ca/) goods than most [Americans](https://www.usa.gov/) realize.” For instance, [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/) exports millions of barrels a day of oil to the upper Midwest and enough electricity to [power some two million homes in Maine](https://www.pressherald.com/2026/08/25/as-canada-trade-war-rages-could-maine-electricity-prices-spike/) – to pick but two examples that may affect key [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) races.

Given these headwinds, it’s little surprise that retail sales fell in July [for the first time in nine months](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-retail-sales-unexpectedly-fall-july-2026-08-14/) as [the economy also lost jobs](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/new-job-numbers-july-data-economy-bls-trump-gdp). Again, [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)s usually get far too much credit or blame for the state of the economy. But in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s case, the causation is much clearer.

And it means a key part of his political image is backfiring. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/), like many a businessman turned politician, has long presented himself as someone who gets stuff done. At the [2016 Republican convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_National_Convention?wprov=sfla1) he pledged [“I alone can fix it”](https://www.theatlantic.com/politics/archive/2016/07/trump-rnc-speech-alone-fix-it/492557/) and he leaned even further into that image as a “man of action” in the 2024 campaign. (Remember [“I will immediately bring prices down, starting on Day One”](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-economy-inflation-gdp-bessent)?) Now he has acted, and voters are realizing they don’t like it.

### "I Put Up Tariffs" - Marsh Family adaptation of Bob Marley and the Wailers, "I Shot the Sheriff"

In 1973, [Bob Marley and the Wailers](https://www.bobmarley.com/) released one of their best-known reggae tracks, “I Shot the Sheriff” – an aching story about justice that tells the story of a violent encounter with the police in [Jamaica](https://www.gov.jm/), and its origin in one man’s unpleasant and unfair actions. It has a wonderful lilt and sway, though built with not that many instruments. We were obviously missing a few – including organ – but figured we’d try to play this live rather than backfill any extras, and get as near as we could. Amidst the sorting and practicing, unfortunately no one noticed Tom was not visible in the shot. So you’ve been spared his groove, though we dropped in a couple of post-cut shots to show you where he was 😊

Although most adaptations have stuck to the same themes, we’ve shifted it across to tell the story of [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s favourite toys: tariffs, which he has already taken action on, and promised to do much more as he shakes up the world order, starting with upsetting his nearest neighbours. The consensus is that the tariffs didn’t do what he claimed they would in his [first term](https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/), and that they are now actively threatening stock prices, confidence, capital expenditure, trade flows, and all out economic war with old and new blocs retaliating. It’s a scene we’ve seen before, hence the reference to the [“Smoot-Hawley” act of 1930](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/COMPS-8183/uslm/COMPS-8183.xml) which raised tariffs on 20,000 US imports and helped prompt a global downturn and deepen the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression?wprov=sfla1).
