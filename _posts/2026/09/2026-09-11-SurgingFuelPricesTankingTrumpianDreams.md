## [Gas prices are surging at the worst possible time for Republicans](https://www.ms.now/news/gas-prices-surging-worst-possible-time-republicans)

_[Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) privately acknowledge that gas prices are becoming a huge issue in the midterms, and [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) is publicly acknowledging that the issue is unlikely to get better anytime soon._

By [Mychael Schnell](https://www.ms.now/author/mychael-schnell) and [Lillie Boudreaux](https://www.ms.now/author/lillie-boudreaux)

Sept. 11, 2026, 5:00 AM EDT

Gas prices are above \$4 a gallon nationally. Diesel prices are pennies away from \$6. Oil is back over \$100 a barrel. There is no end in sight for the [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) war. And the midterm elections are less than two months away.

Gas prices have been elevated ever since the beginning of the [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) war in February, but costs at the pump had eased somewhat as the [United States](https://www.usa.gov/) negotiated a peace deal with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) over the summer.

Now, with that peace deal looking more like a distant dream, prices are on the rise again — \$4.27 a gallon nationally — crossing an important psychological threshold at the very moment that voters begin to cast their ballots.

For [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/), the timing couldn’t be worse.

Privately, [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmakers acknowledge that the high gas prices are a huge challenge. [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) note that [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) campaigned on relieving pain at the pump, with one telling [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/), “Lowering fuel prices was a big campaign promise.”

Now, those gas prices are crossing $4 throughout the U.S., and there is a new economic issue for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) to defend just as voters see the issue getting worse, not better.

“Across the country, it’s going to be a problem,” the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker said.

Another [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Republican](https://www.gop.com/), who requested anonymity, said the recent rise in fuel costs is “challenging” and “concerning,” noting that it has become a “topic of discussion” among [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/). A third [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker, also speaking anonymously about private fears, said the threat posed by a recent spike in fuel costs is “real.”

“People still vote with their pocketbook and it’s gonna be in the back of people’s mind,” the lawmaker said.

And one [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker, [Rep.](https://www.house.gov/) [Tim Burchett (T-TN2)](https://burchett.house.gov/), was transparent about the political potency of gas prices: “If the [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) were in power, I would use it on them.”

The concern for the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) is that high gas prices are here to stay, and that prices could actually climb higher as voting begins.

Lawmakers and operatives from both parties say the balance of power in the [House](https://www.house.gov/) and [Senate](https://www.senate.gov/) could very likely hinge on gas prices. If [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) can get prices back below \$4 a gallon, there’s a much better chance the issue fades into the background and voters return to their traditional partisan fox holes. But if gas prices start with a \$4 — and if diesel moves above \$6 — [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) see an opportunity to reach voters who haven’t traditionally sided with them, or to simply discourage some key [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) voters from coming out in November.

On Wednesday, as he departed for the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/)’s [midterm convention](https://www.gopconvention.com/) in [Dallas](https://dallascityhall.com/), [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) insisted gas prices would come “tumbling down.”

“I think for gasoline, we’ll get them below \$2 a gallon,” he said.

But he acknowledged that it would likely take “a little bit longer than the midterms” for prices to come down.

That is little comfort to [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) running for re-election now. As [GasBuddy](https://www.gasbuddy.com/) petroleum analyst [Patrick De Haan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gasbuddyguy/) said, even if oil started coming down today, “It might take a few weeks to a couple months because prices have gone up so high.” 

“The higher they go, the longer it takes them to come back down to normal,” [De Haan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/gasbuddyguy/) told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

And inconveniently for [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/), oil prices are headed in the opposite direction.

Brent crude oil exceeded \$100 a barrel on Wednesday as fighting between the U.S. and [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) escalated and [Ukraine](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/) struck [Russian](http://kremlin.ru/) oil refineries. Gas prices rose to a national average of \$4.27 a gallon, and diesel prices hit a record-high of \$5.97 this week. Diesel is on track to pass $6 a gallon for the first time in history.

Because [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) tried to soften the blow earlier in the year by releasing barrels from the [Strategic Petroleum Reserve](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/opr/strategic-petroleum-reserve), the [U.S. SPR](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/opr/strategic-petroleum-reserve) is near its lowest level since 1982. The [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/) doesn’t have as many levers to lower prices.

On top of it all, the high diesel costs have a “disproportional impact on rural households and businesses,” [Adie Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/), a senior fellow at the [Brookings Institution](https://www.brookings.edu/), told [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/).

“It’s hard to see how communities rooted deeply in farming and agriculture and heavy industry will not have this economic news top of mind,” [Tomer](https://www.brookings.edu/people/adie-tomer/) said.

Farmers and cattle ranchers are already struggling from the impacts of [Trump’s tariff war](https://www.ms.now/news/canadas-tariffs-hit-tuesday-but-their-effect-on-the-midterms-is-just-beginning) and his decision to import [foreign beef](https://www.ms.now/news/republican-lawmakers-congress-trump-beef). ([Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s support among rural Americans dropped to a near record low of 44%, according to a [July Faux News poll](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/fox-news-poll-voters-oppose-iran-war-see-prolonged-commitment) poll.)

One of the vulnerable [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) who spoke to [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) noted that farmers tend to lean toward [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/).

“But is there a lot of frustration? Yes,” the lawmaker said.

Another [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker recalled a recent conversation with a farmer who was “complaining about the diesel fuel prices.”

And [Burchett](https://burchett.house.gov/) agreed that the recent rise in prices has hit farmers particularly hard.

“It’s tough on them,” he said.

The actual impact of rising gas prices is a key question. Some [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) worry it could be the ballgame, while others don’t “think” it will cost the [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) the election.

“I pray that our voters don’t use this as the only thing that they care about when determining the future of the country,” another [GOP](https://www.gop.com/) lawmaker said.

Another one of the previously quoted [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) said they wished the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) “would do a better job of messaging and explaining to the American people the challenges of making sure that [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) does not have a nuclear weapon.”

Rhetorical challenges aside, [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) have little time to turn back the tide — and there’s no real indication that things are getting better.

One of the [House](https://www.house.gov/) [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) who spoke to [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/) said that “if things were to stay calm” from now until November, the current gas prices may be “baked in at this point.”

But the [Republican](https://www.gop.com/) warned that if [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) “continues to go after other countries” — like with the recent [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/) tariffs — it will be “a problem.”

“Because now you’re making the situation worse, and voters are gonna see that,” they said.

“He can defend his situation in [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), the war there, and his reasoning. And they might not agree with it, but at least he’s doing this,” the lawmaker continued. “But at this point, trying to drag other countries into a conflict and then basically punishing our consumers along the way is not gonna go over well. And I think that’ll go beyond anything that is baked in.”

“I think it’ll make it worse,” the battleground lawmaker said. “For voters and for consumers in general.”
