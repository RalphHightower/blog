## [Diesel costs $6 a gallon for the first time ever. Here’s how that will hurt you.](https://www.ms.now/opinion/diesel-costs-economy-inflation)

_Diesel powers everything from construction equipment to tractors, which means higher prices._

By [Charlie Herman](https://wwnw.ms.now/author/charlie-herman) and [Ryan Teague Beckwith](https://www.ms.now/author/ryan-teague-beckwith)

Sept. 12, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT

Unless you got your first driver’s license before the 1990s, you’ve probably never [owned](https://afdc.energy.gov/vehicle-registration) a diesel car. But the price of diesel still affects your daily life.

Diesel is the [workhorse](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-08/war-brings-winter-of-discontent-for-the-world-s-workhorse-fuel) of the American economy. When a tractor plows a field, that’s diesel. When a truck carries freshly picked strawberries to the grocery store, that’s diesel, too. The school bus taking your kids to school, the bulldozer demolishing a house down the street and the dump truck hauling away your garbage: all diesel.

Gas prices get more attention, but [diesel matters](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/26/business/energy-environment/diesel-prices-iran-war-oil.html) just as much for the broader [economy](https://www.ms.now/news/news-analysis/us-diesel-gas-prices-record-labor-day-vance). And right now, the outlook is grim. Prices are up more than 63% from a year ago and have set new records every day this week. On Friday diesel cost more than \$6 for the first time on record, reaching a national average of \$6.056 a gallon, according to [AAA](https://gasprices.aaa.com/). You may not see those increases at your own pump, but you will eventually see them at the grocery store.

Unlike a spike in gasoline, which hits drivers immediately, higher diesel costs take time to filter through the economy. Manufacturers and shippers pass them along; [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/), [UPS](https://www.ups.com/) and [FedEx](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/home.html) have already added [fuel surcharges](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/ups-amazon-fuel-surcharges-help-blunt-higher-costs/826840/). That means the latest increase is likely to keep pushing up prices for months.

[President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) acknowledged Wednesday that he does not expect oil prices to fall before the midterms, saying they would start “[tumbling downward](https://apnews.com/article/trump-oil-prices-iran-midterms-22ffe753bb2d60c99024ca6cfbec4142)” only afterward. He tied that prediction to a hoped-for end to the war with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/), which has disrupted oil flows through the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1). But for families and businesses facing higher costs now, that’s cold comfort, even if it proves to be true.

The outlook ahead does not look much better. The [Energy Department](https://www.energy.gov/)’s latest forecast estimates that diesel prices will be [8.2% higher next year](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf) than it projected just last month. It also warns that households using heating oil could pay more this winter. And that forecast was issued before diesel began setting these new records.

### Limited-time offer
During a nearly two-hour speech Wednesday night, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) offered a modern version of Herbert [Hoover’s](https://politicaldictionary.com/words/chicken-in-every-pot/) “chicken in every pot”: a \$5,000 “dividend” for every American adult if [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) retain control of [Congress](https://www.congress.gov/). As he continues [trying](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/2026/10/big-lie-local-elections-midterms/688359/) to [muck](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/08/us/politics/trump-mail-voting-what-to-know-supreme-court.html) [up](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/08/25/trump-is-having-trouble-changing-how-americans-vote/) [voting](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/27/trump-voting-agenda-mail-ballots-save-act-voter-rolls), the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) is also offering voters cash to keep his party in power.

Whether the proposal is legal or would become law, it could cost more than [\$1 trillion](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/10/us/politics/trump-dividend-5000-dollar-checks.html), adding to the \$40 trillion national debt. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) suggested the money could come from tariff revenue, but tariffs are bringing in [less than \$200 billion a year](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-much-revenue-does-the-federal-government-collect-from-tariffs/country/united-states/) a year. Plus, he has already [committed](https://www.cato.org/blog/tariffs-funded-everything-2025-will-fantasy-continue-2026) that revenue to a “dream military,” aid for farmers, enhanced childcare and, wait for it, a separate \$2,000 “tariff dividend.”

### But wait, there’s more
Our colleague [Catherine Rampell](https://www.linkedin.com/in/catherinerampell/) [summed](https://x.com/crampell/status/2097872175654989939?s=46) up [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s recent promises:

- \$5,000 stimulus checks through a “[DOGE](https://doge.gov/) dividend”
- \$2,000 stimulus checks through a “tariff dividend”
- \$1,000-plus checks from “savings” created by expiring Obamacare subsidies
- A new \$5,000 “dividend” if [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) win the midterms
(Maybe someone should tell [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](https://x.com/SarahHuckabee/status/2098048049087934580?s=20) what her former boss is proposing.)

Most of the checks [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has promised Americans have not happened yet. But if [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) really wants to put more money in Americans’ pockets, one option would be to cancel his tariffs. He moved in that direction when he announced he would [lift tariffs](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/further-ensuring-affordable-beef-for-the-american-consumer/?) on [imported beef](https://www.ms.now/rachel-maddow-show/maddowblog/trump-beef-imports-tariffs-mystery-meat-industry-brazil-argentina) in an effort to [to bring prices down](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/21/trump-ground-beef-import-tariff.html). Current tariffs are expected to cost the average American household an additional [$820](https://taxfoundation.org/research/federal-tax/trump-tariffs-trade-war/?) to [$1,100](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs?) this year.

### But ‘affordability’ is still ‘fake’
“They used the word affordability, right? It’s fake,” the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) said Wednesday night. “They are so fake because it was them that caused the affordability, to use their word, crisis.” He added that he’s bringing prices “way down, way way down.” Tell that to anyone who has to drive. Or has gone to the grocery store. Or has tried to buy a house.

### Bond market 1, Bessent 0
The [Treasury Department](https://home.treasury.gov/) on Wednesday [announced](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/09/business/economy/treasury-repurchase-bessent.html) that it would [purchase](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/bessent-treasury-bonds-repurchase-rcna596819) \$6 billion in long-term bonds, tripling its previous commitment and more than doubling its regular purchases in an effort to lower borrowing costs.

The market’s [response](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/sep/09/treasury-bond-buyback?)? Rather than falling, yields on [10-year Treasurys](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-notes/) rose, reaching their [highest level](https://www.wsj.com/finance/investing/bessents-latest-buyback-move-leaves-investors-wanting-more-82d0268b?mod=hp_lead_pos4) in almost three years. Translation: [Treasury]() [Secretary Scott Bessent](https://home.treasury.gov/about/general-information/officials/scott-bessent) tried to “slow things down,” and borrowing costs moved in the other direction for American businesses and consumers. Mortgage rates soon followed, with the average 30-year fixed rate [jumping above 7%](https://www.mortgagenewsdaily.com/mortgage-rates/30-year-fixed?) for the first time in almost 16 months.

### ‘Come with me if you want to live’
Nearly 48 hours after former [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) and [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) researcher Jacob Coxon [posted his warning](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843) that people developing artificial intelligence fear it could kill us all, his post had been viewed nearly 158 million times. [Two](https://x.com/EvanHub/status/2097497037956891126) [current](https://x.com/saprmarks/status/2097570226804011302) [Anthropic](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/09/anthropic-researchers-ai-human-extinction?) employees subsequently echoed his concerns.

In a statement, [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) said it has been transparent about AI’s “enormous benefits and unprecedented risks” and is building models with what it called some of the industry’s strongest safeguards.

_How could this AI-versus-humans scenario happen?_ 

The [Wall Street Journal lays it out.](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/how-would-ai-actually-kill-us-all-what-to-know-about-the-ai-doomsday-debate-034270d1?mod=hp_lead_pos4) lays it out. The concern is that AI systems could ignore or push back against the controls humans impose on them. Researchers have already documented instances in which AI systems took steps to avoid being shut down. Taken to the extreme, a misaligned model could treat human safety as an obstacle to accomplishing its goals.

_Why the sudden increase in concern?_ 

There are recent [examples](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/sep/08/openai-rogue-models-hugging-face-investigation) of AI programs hacking other companies’ systems and trying to cover their tracks.

As [some](https://x.com/JohnCassidy/status/2097707667699405259) have pointed out, [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) and [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/) may be caught in a “[prisoner’s dilemma](https://progressless.org/2023/05/31/could-ai-be-an-example-of-the-prisoners-dilemma/).” Each company is under pressure to move faster than the other for fear of falling behind, even as both companies — including their [CEOs](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/how-would-ai-actually-kill-us-all-what-to-know-about-the-ai-doomsday-debate-034270d1?mod=hp_lead_pos4) — acknowledge that the consequences could be [catastrophic](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/09/anthropic-researchers-ai-human-extinction?).

### Data centers can’t catch a break
Voter backlash to data centers — and concern about AI’s broader risks — has prompted states to reconsider the generous tax breaks they have offered developers. The [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/politics/policy/states-that-gave-data-centers-billions-in-tax-breaks-are-now-ripping-up-the-deals-4879c4f9?mod=hp_lead_pos1&) reports that more than 35 states offer sales tax exemptions or similar incentives to data center developers.

Some industry officials have responded by suggesting that they could move projects to states that still offer tax incentives. But a multimillion-dollar physical data center cannot simply pick up and move once construction is underway.

### Breaking records … for prices
The [record-setting](https://sports.yahoo.com/tennis/article/us-open-ben-shelton-powers-through-carlos-alcaraz-in-historic-late-night-thriller-073519676.html) match between Ben Shelton and Carlos Alcaraz ended at 3:33 a.m. Wednesday, giving those still in the stands plenty for their money. That may be some consolation, given the price of attending.

The [New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7524782/2026/08/20/us-open-ticket-prices-grounds-passes-courtside-live-sports-cost/) noted the disconnect between a tournament held in a public park, on public courts and run by a nonprofit intended to expand access to tennis — and its rapidly rising prices. The ongoing \$800 million renovation has put a greater premium on [luxury hospitality](https://www.townandcountrymag.com/leisure/sporting/a73572342/arthur-ashe-stadium-premium-hospitality-2026/) for fans with [money to spend](https://www.curbed.com/article/us-open-arthur-ashe-stadium.html). Meanwhile, [affordable tickets](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/31/opinion/us-open-prices.html) are often quickly snapped up by bots and scalpers, who then resell them at higher prices.
