## [The biggest hurdle for Democrats hoping to investigate Trump next year](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-house-democrats-investigations-counsel)

_The new [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) counsel has plenty of experience delaying efforts to hold [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) accountable._

By [Hayes Brown](https://www.ms.now/author/hayes-brown)

Sept. 6, 2026, 6:00 AM EDT

Even [Republicans](https://www.gop.com/) seem to think [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) are poised to capture control of [at least one chamber of Congress](https://www.ms.now/news/news-analysis/senate-race-ratings-project-47) during the midterm elections. Consequently, [Will Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/), [took over as White House counsel](https://www.ms.now/news/the-governments-top-three-lawyers-share-something-in-common-they-were-all-on-donald-trumps-legal-team) this week, is reportedly leading other [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) lawyers in battening down the hatches against [an expected onslaught of congressional investigations](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/01/white-house-prepares-democratic-congress-01060639).

[Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/), like so many of the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s top-ranking lawyers, [formerly worked as one of President Donald Trump’s personal lawyers](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-lawyers-justice-alina-habba). That’s a problem, because there is a line between serving the presidency and serving the man who holds the office. There are times when that line can be fuzzy, but [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) has encouraged his [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) to act as if there isn’t one at all.

Many of the individuals in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s orbit are reportedly looking to lawyer up.

According to a recent [New York Times profile of Scharf](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/26/us/politics/will-scharf-white-house-counsel.html), “friends and colleagues who know him well say that he does not see standing up to Mr. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) as part of his job.” That’s arguably true for his former role as [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) staff secretary, but it doesn’t bode well for him encouraging cooperation with investigations that target [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) or his family. A former [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) official told [Politico](https://www.politico.com/) that “they expect [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/) to play a bit more to what the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) wants’” than his immediate predecessor as counsel, [David Warrington](https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-warrington-21807916/).

Many of the individuals in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s orbit are reportedly looking to lawyer up and are hoping that [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/) efforts to [extend the scope of executive privilege](https://www.ms.now/news/trumps-doj-plans-to-treat-his-communications-with-outside-advisors-as-privileged-why-now) to communication with private advisers will shield them from congressional subpoenas. [The Washington Post reported in May](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/05/04/white-house-briefs-staff-midterm-losses/) in May that the counsel’s office, then run by [Warrington](https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-warrington-21807916/), was giving political appointees 30-minute [PowerPoint](https://powerpoint.cloud.microsoft/en-us/) presentations about congressional oversight. According to the [Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/), staffers were also “encouraged … to be careful about what they put in writing and provided guidance for how to respond to congressional inquiries in a timely manner.”

During the first [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) legal team was initially focused on blunting the effects of the [Russia](http://kremlin.ru/) probe and accordingly was slow to react to [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) winning the [House](https://www.house.gov/) in 2018. That is, the attorneys were unprepared for the wave of requests for documents and testimony from staffers. [Pat Cipollone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Cipollone?wprov=sfla1), then [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) counsel, [a hard-line approach against cooperating with the inquiry](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/white-house-rejects-congress-demands-says-investigations-amount-unauthorized-do-n1006016), which culminated in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s first impeachment. House [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) eventually [charged Trump with abusing his power](https://www.congress.gov/116/bills/hres755/BILLS-116hres755enr.pdf) in a scheme to influence the [2020 election](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections2020.pdf) and obstructing their investigation.

That said, [Cipollone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Cipollone?wprov=sfla1)’s efforts to [stonewall and stymie those probes](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-impeachment-inquiry/white-house-refuses-turn-over-documents-democrats-impeachment-inquiry-n1063771) were largely successful. Most of the evidence provided in the impeachment inquiry came from career staffers who testified over the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/)’s objections. The executive branch turned over almost no evidence willingly. But even [Cipollone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Cipollone?wprov=sfla1) and his top deputy refused to go along with [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s efforts to overturn the [2020 election](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections2020.pdf), and they [cooperated with the House Jan. 6 Committee’s probe](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/former-trump-white-house-counsel-pat-cipollone-cooperative-jan-6-commi-rcna37320).

It’s hard to say if similar limits exist for the cadre of lawyers who defended [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) against the indictments that followed his attempted coup. Many of them have been [rewarded handsomely for their success](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/05/01/trump-lawyers-administration-judges-00902690). Besides [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/), that includes [Attorney General](https://www.justice.gov/) [Todd Blanche](https://www.justice.gov/ag/staff-profile/meet-attorney-general) and [Solicitor General](https://www.justice.gov/osg) [John Sauer](https://www.justice.gov/osg/staff-profile/solicitor-general-john-sauer). ([Emil Bove](https://www.fjc.gov/history/judges/bove-emil-joseph-iii), who was for a time the No. 3 at the [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/), is now a [federal appeals court judge](https://www.ca3.uscourts.gov/).) Even though none of these former personal attorneys still work for him directly, congressional [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) would be wise to see a similar effort to halt their probes’ momentum.

Before he was tapped as counsel, [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/) served as [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) staff secretary, essentially managing the flow of documents that reached [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s desk. He showed a glimpse of willingness to push back against his most extreme colleagues in that role.  [He wrote memos for chief of staff Susie Wiles](https://www.ms.now/opinion/trump-first-year-blitzkrieg-stalled-out) laying out the problems with proposals to suspend habeas corpus for immigrant detainees and invoke the [Insurrection Act](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title10/subtitleA/part1/chapter13&edition=prelim) against protesters. But there’s a difference between disagreeing with someone else in the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) and saying no to the [president](https://www.whitehouse.gov/).

[Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) must employ speed and aggression and dole out real consequences to counteract the delays and drawn-out legal proceedings that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) can be expected to use.

It’s worth noting that [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/) formerly wore a second hat in the [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) as chair of the [National Capital Planning Commission](https://www.ncpc.gov/). He [happily rubber-stamped approval](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/02/us/politics/trump-ballroom-commission-vote.html) for [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s multitude of construction projects, seemingly with little regard for laws or norms. His new role as [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) counsel mandates that [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/) be less of a yes-man and strictly adhere to the law, but given his past history in [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s employ, the odds don’t seem great.

As he and other [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) officials are reportedly expecting [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) to investigate the [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/), those congressional [Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) would do well to review the tactics people on [Team Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/), including [Scharf](https://www.linkedin.com/in/will-scharf-35214a10/) himself, have previously used to avoid exposure or accountability. The [success of those tactics](https://www.ms.now/opinion/msnbc-opinion/trump-lawyers-delay-sentencing-trials-election-day-rcna169998) helped [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) reach the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) again.

[Democrats](https://www.democrats.org/) must employ speed and aggression and dole out real consequences to counteract the delays and drawn-out legal proceedings that [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) can be expected to use as he again tries to avoid accountability for his assault on democracy.
