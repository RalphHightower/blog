## [Signalgate was all about this militant group. Now they’re threatening to cost Trump the midterms.](https://www.ms.now/news/houthi-militants-red-sea-midterms-signalgate)

_The [Iranian](https://irangov.ir/)-backed [Houthi militants](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) are once again threatening oil shipment routes through the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1). What will [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) do this time?

By [Felicia Schwartz](https://www.ms.now/author/felicia-schwartz)

Sept. 18, 2026, 5:00 AM EDT

The U.S. decision to attack the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) last year unwittingly kicked off one of the most bruising political scandals of [President](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) [Donald Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s second term: [Signalgate](https://signal.org/).

Now the [Iranian](https://irangov.ir/)-backed [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) are once again wreaking havoc on vital [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1) transit routes, with [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) holding back and deciding not to widen the [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) campaign — for now.

American diplomats met with [Houthi representatives](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) at the [U.S. Embassy](https://om.usembassy.gov/) in [Oman](https://gov.om/) over the weekend, a source briefed on the meeting said.

The goal was to cool down the conflict and ensure that ships can pass through the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1), said the source, who was granted anonymity to describe sensitive discussions.

Both sides reaffirmed their commitment to a ceasefire set last year — in which the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) agreed not to hit U.S. targets and vice versa.

Much has changed since spring of last year, when the United States waged a seven-week, more than \$1 billion military campaign to restore safe passage in the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1) — one top [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) officials, including [Defense](https://www.defense.gov/) [Secretary Pete Hegseth](https://www.defense.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4040890/hon-pete-hegseth/), discussed on the encrypted phone app [Signal](https://signal.org/).

The chats were made public after then-U.S. national security adviser [Mike Wartz](https://usun.usmission.gov/ambassador-michael-waltz/) mistakenly added a journalist, sparking months of chaos and staff shake-ups.

[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/), frustrated with the lack of progress at the time, halted the operation in May 2025 and reached a truce with the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/). The [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/), an [Iran](https://irangov.ir/)-backed militant group in eastern [Yemen](https://pmo-ye.net/), agreed not to attack U.S. vessels.

But now, the [Houthi](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) threat has morphed.

The group is seizing opportunity as the U.S.-[Iran](https://irangov.ir/) war grinds on and [Tehran](https://tehran.ir/) maintains control of the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1). [Saudi Arabia](https://my.gov.sa/) had rerouted much of its oil exports through the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1) to avoid that choke point, but the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) are now threatening its backup route, too, pushing up oil prices just as the U.S. midterm elections near.

[Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman](https://houseofsaud.com/saudi-royal-family-profiles/prince-mohammed-bin-salman-al-saud/) last week asked [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) to strike the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) after the group seized control of the [Bab al-Mandeb Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Bab_al-Mandab?wprov=sfla1), a key oil transit route on the other side of the [Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula?wprov=sfla1) from the [Strait of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz?wprov=sfla1) that connects the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1) and the [Gulf of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aden?wprov=sfla1), according to a person familiar with the call who was granted anonymity to discuss sensitive diplomacy. [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) declined the request, telling the crown prince that the U.S. wants [Riyadh](https://www.alriyadh.gov.sa/) to take the lead in what the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) sees as a regional security challenge, the person said.

Asked about the call while in Ireland on Sept. 12, [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) said the crown prince is “a good friend of mine” and “everything’s going to work out fine and dandy.”

Despite [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s optimism, Americans have been increasingly critical of his handling of the war and the economy, with the Houthi escalation threatening to further spike high gas prices and pull the U.S. more deeply into the region, though [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) and his team are trying to avoid it.

The [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) are now again attacking international shipping in the [Red Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea?wprov=sfla1) — largely targeting ships exporting oil from Saudi Arabia — and have seized control of key ports in the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

The [Yemen](https://pmo-ye.net/)-based militant group has so far steered clear of American vessels, giving the U.S. cover not to attack Houthi targets for now.

The U.S. expects the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) to continue upholding the terms of last year’s ceasefire, a senior [administration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/) official said, adding that the group has communicated to the U.S. that “they don’t want to fight with us.”

[Washington](https://dc.gov/) is “empowering our regional partners to take the lead in managing and resolving regional security challenges,” the official said.

Last year’s ceasefire with the [Houthis](https://ansarollah.com.ye/) “takes the U.S. out of the equation,” said David [Schenker](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/experts/david-schenker), who was the top Middle East official at the [State Department](https://www.state.gov/) during [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/)’s first term.

“[Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) doesn’t want to start another campaign, certainly not before the elections,” said [Schenker](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/experts/david-schenker), who is now a fellow at the [Washington Institute](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/). “We’re short of munitions and already stretched.”

That rationale is frustrating to [Saudi Arabia](https://houseofsaud.com/saudi-royal-family-profiles/prince-mohammed-bin-salman-al-saud/) and [Washington](https://dc.gov/)’s other Middle East partners, who did not want [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) to launch a war with [Tehran](https://tehran.ir/) and now must face the security consequences as [Trump](https://www.donaldjtrump.com/) struggles to find an offramp and diplomacy flounders, analysts said.

“The war with [Iran](https://irangov.ir/) really exposed the vulnerabilities of a lot of our partners in the Gulf,” [Schenker](https://www.washingtoninstitute.org/experts/david-schenker) said. “It raises all sorts of questions about what a U.S. security commitment to the Gulf looks like.”

[Felicia Schwartz](https://www.ms.now/author/felicia-schwartz) is a national security reporter for MS NOW.
